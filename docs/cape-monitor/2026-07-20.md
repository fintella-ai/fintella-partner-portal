## 🚨 Major Changes Found

### 1. CAPE Phase 2 — LIVE since June 29, 2026

**What changed:** Phase 2 of CAPE launched on June 29, 2026 per CBP confirmation at the June 9, 2026 CIT hearing.

**Scope:** Entries of types **01** (Consumption), **02** (Consumption Quota/Visa), and **06** (Consumption FTZ) that are **flagged for reconciliation** where the **Type 09 Reconciliation Summary has NOT yet been filed**.

**Key details:**
- Same 80-day liquidation window as Phase 1 (unliquidated + liquidated within 80 days)
- CAPE removes IEEPA duties from flagged entries *prior* to the reconciliation entry being filed — separating the IEEPA refund from reconciliation calculations
- Type 09 itself (the reconciliation summary entry) **remains excluded** from CAPE — it is not a filing mechanism, only the underlying flagged entries become eligible
- Drawback, ADCVD (unliquidated), entries under protest, non-ACE entries, and entries where reconciliation entry has already been filed remain excluded from Phase 2

**Code impact:** No change to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` (Type 09 stays in; types 01/02/06 were already not excluded). Added `isFlaggedForReconciliation` field to `EntryForEligibility`, `"cape_phase2"` to `FilingMethod` type, and routing logic in `checkEligibility()` to correctly classify these entries.

### 2. CAPE Phase 3 — Targeting End of July 2026 (CIT Litigants Only)

**What's coming:** Phase 3 will cover **finally liquidated entries** (liquidated > 80 days ago, representing ~$11.4B in IEEPA duties). CBP confirmed at the June 9, 2026 CIT hearing that Phase 3 tooling will be ready by end of July 2026.

**Critical restriction:** The DOJ filed a motion on May 29, 2026 asserting CBP lacks authority to issue refunds on finally liquidated entries without an importer-specific court judgment. Phase 3 refunds will only flow to the **~3,700 importers who have filed suits at the CIT**.

**Litigation context:**
- CIT plans to ORDER reliquidation of finally liquidated entries for CIT litigants once Phase 3 launches
- Government is appealing to the Federal Circuit — ruling could restrict or expand access
- CBP's May 29 position contradicts CBP's own FAQ page (which previously indicated finally liquidated entries would be covered in upcoming phases) and the January 2026 concession to "all current and future similarly situated plaintiffs"
- Non-litigants may need to file individual CIT cases to qualify

**Code impact (current session):** No change — Phase 3 is not live and restricted to CIT litigants. `filingMethod: "litigation"` for entries >180 days past liquidation is correct. Updated JSDoc on `FilingMethod` to note the Phase 3 context.

**Action needed when Phase 3 goes live:** Evaluate whether to add `"cape_phase3_litigation"` to `FilingMethod` and surface a flag for CIT-litigant clients.

## Advisory Findings (No Code Change Required)

### 7. Silent IOR Rejection Bug — NEW (operator advisory)
**Source:** Trade compliance community reporting (April–July 2026)

Customs brokers filing on behalf of **multiple Importers of Record** in a single CAPE CSV submission are experiencing silent rejections: CAPE processes only the first IOR's entries and silently drops the rest without an error message.

**Impact:** A broker filing a CSV with entries from IOR A and IOR B will receive refunds only for IOR A. IOR B's entries are dropped with no rejection notice.

**Workaround:** Submit **one CSV file per IOR**. Our `generateCapeCsv()` does not currently group by IOR (the `EntryForCape` interface does not carry an IOR field). If we add IOR-aware CSV generation in the future, this will need attention.

### 8. Rejection Rate — Stable at ~15%
Four dominant Phase 1 rejection patterns account for ~79% of all rejections:
1. HTS classification mismatches
2. Valuation inconsistencies
3. Country-of-origin discrepancies
4. Section 232/301 stacking errors

No CAPE system outages reported. No change from last known rate (~15%).
