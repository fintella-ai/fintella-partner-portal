## 6. Section 122 — MAJOR EXTERNAL DEVELOPMENT (Monitor Closely)

This is the most significant development since Phase 1 launch.

**May 7, 2026**: The U.S. Court of International Trade (CIT) ruled that the Trump administration's 10% global ad valorem tariffs imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (effective ~late February 2026) **exceed the president's statutory authority** and are unlawful.

**However, a temporary stay is in effect.** The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit issued a stay freezing the CIT ruling, pending appeal. The government's deadline to formally appeal is approximately **June 7, 2026**.

**Impact on `tariff-calculator.ts`**:
- The `section122` rate type in `RateLookupResult.breakdown` is **correct** — Section 122 rates should remain in the calculator
- Section 122 refunds are **NOT processed via CAPE** — CAPE covers only IEEPA duties. Section 122 refunds (if the ruling stands and the stay lifts) would be a separate CBP process not yet announced
- Once the June 7 appeal deadline passes, re-evaluate whether to add a `section122_refund_eligible` flag to `EligibilityResult` and a corresponding routing bucket

**Action at June 7, 2026**: Run a follow-up monitor check. If appeal not filed (or denied), expect CBP to issue Section 122 refund guidance and a separate portal/process — will likely require a new entry type or rate type handling in the calculator.

## 9. Secondary Market for Refund Rights (Informational)

A secondary market is developing where IORs sell their IEEPA refund rights to investors for immediate liquidity (rather than waiting 60–90 days for CBP processing). This is legally complex. Key risk: government has until June 7, 2026 to appeal the original IEEPA CIT ruling — an appeal + stay could suspend payments. Fintella partners should be informed that CBP refunds go directly to the IOR via ACH and **cannot be redirected to a third party without IOR action**.
