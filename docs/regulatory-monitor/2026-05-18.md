## Key Findings

### 1. CIT Strikes Down Section 122 Tariffs — Federal Circuit Stays Ruling ⚠️ WATCH

**What happened:**  
On **May 7, 2026**, the U.S. Court of International Trade (2-1) held that Proclamation 11012 — the 10% global import surcharge imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 — was **unlawful** because the proclamation failed to identify the specific type of "balance-of-payments deficits" required by the statute. The court entered a permanent injunction against collection of Section 122 duties *for the three named plaintiffs only* (Burlap & Barrel, Inc.; Basic Fun, Inc.; State of Washington).

On **May 12, 2026**, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit issued an **administrative stay** suspending the CIT's order while the government's appeal is briefed. Expedited schedule in place; further ruling expected within weeks.

**Impact on calculator:**  
- Section 122 tariffs are **still being collected** for all non-plaintiff importers while the stay holds.
- CAPE Phase 1 refunds IEEPA duties only (Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026). Section 122 duties are entirely separate and not subject to IEEPA refunds. The `checkEligibility()` function correctly excludes post-Feb-23-2026 entries.
- The S122-01 seed entry (10%, expires Jul 24, 2026) remains accurate.
- **No calculator code or seed data changes needed today.**

**Why this matters:**  
If the Federal Circuit upholds the CIT ruling, Section 122 tariffs would be unlawful — potentially creating a SECOND wave of refund claims (March 2026–onward). That would be a Phase 3+ CAPE scenario. Monitor for Federal Circuit ruling.
