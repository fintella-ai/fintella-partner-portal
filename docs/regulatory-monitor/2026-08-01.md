## Change 1 — CRITICAL: Section 301 Forced Labor Tariff Replaced Section 122 (July 24, 2026)

**What changed:** The Section 122 global 10% surcharge expired at its statutory 150-day limit at 12:01 AM ET July 24, 2026. USTR announced a final Section 301 forced labor tariff action on July 23, 2026, effective at the exact moment of Section 122's expiration. No tariff gap.

**New rate structure (two-tier):**
- **10%** — 17 economies USTR found have forced labor import prohibitions (Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, European Union, Indonesia, Mexico, Pakistan + 8 others per FR 2026-15181)
- **12.5%** — 43 economies with NO forced labor import prohibition (China, India, UK, Vietnam, and ~40 others)
- **No statutory expiration** (unlike Section 122's 150-day cap)
- **Legal authority:** Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. § 2411)
- **HTS codes:** 9903.05.xx (10% tier), 9903.06.xx (12.5% tier); 101 new Chapter 99 headings under U.S. Note 52
- **CBP guidance:** CSMS #69326983
- **Federal Register:** 2026-15181 (published July 28, 2026)
- **USTR announcement:** July 23, 2026

**Calculator impact:** Section 301 tariffs are NOT refundable via CAPE — CAPE covers only IEEPA duties. The seed data update is informational, documenting the current tariff landscape for users. The eligibility logic in `checkEligibility()` is unaffected.

**Files changed:**
- `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json`: Updated `S122-01.supersededBy` and `notes`; added `S301-10PCT` and `S301-125PCT` rate entries; added `section301ForcedLaborCodes` to `htsCodeReference`
- `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`: Added `section301?: number` to `RateLookupResult.breakdown` interface and handling in `lookupCombinedRate()`

## Change 2 — IRS Q3 2026 Interest Rate Increased to 7%

**What changed:** The IRS overpayment interest rate for Q3 2026 (July 1 – September 30, 2026) rose from 6% (Q2 2026) back to 7% (matching all of 2025 and Q1 2026). Federal short-term rate for April 2026 was 4% (+3 = 7%). The rate increase affects interest calculations on IEEPA refunds disbursed or accruing after July 1, 2026.

**Q3 2026 rates:**
- Individual/non-corporate overpayment: **7%**
- Corporate overpayment: **6%**
- Large corporate overpayment (>$10k): **4.5%**
- Underpayment: **7%**
- Large corporate underpayment: **9%**

**Calculator impact:** The seed data only had Q2 2026 (ending June 30) as the last entry. Any refund interest calculation extending past July 1, 2026 was missing Q3 rate data. Added Q3 2026 entry to `irsInterestRates.quarters`.

## Update 3 — CAPE Phase 2 Launched June 29, 2026

**What happened:** CBP launched CAPE Phase 2 on June 29, 2026 (per CSMS #69066837). Phase 2 covers entries flagged for reconciliation (Types 01, 02, 06) where the corresponding Type 09 reconciliation entry has NOT yet been filed.

**Key note:** As of July 3, 2026, ~4.36 million entries failed under Phase 2 due to CBP ACE system issues. CBP issued corrective guidance.

## Update 4 — CAPE Phase 3 (Finally Liquidated Entries) Imminent

**What happened:** CBP confirmed at a June 9, 2026 CIT hearing that CAPE Phase 3 technical programming would be ready by end of July 2026. On July 15, 2026, CIT Judge Richard K. Eaton issued an order directing CBP to reliquidate certain finally liquidated entries for importers with active CIT cases. The order is being entered across ~3,700 pending IEEPA cases.

**Critical limitation:** Phase 3 refunds are currently ONLY for importers who have filed individual lawsuits at the CIT. The government (DOJ) is contesting the legal basis for refunds on finally liquidated entries and has appealed the CIT order to the Federal Circuit.

**CBP next steps:** Progress report due August 4, 2026; closed conference August 5, 2026.

**Calculator impact:** The `litigation` filing method for entries liquidated >180 days ago remains correct for the general population. No code change needed. Added `capePhase3` metadata block to seed data. Importers with active CIT cases should alert their counsel for Phase 3 eligibility.
