## 5. Euro-Notions Florida CIT — Finally Liquidated Entries

**Checked:** Multiple trade law alerts and CIT docket summary

**Finding — WATCH ITEM (no code change yet):**

The Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. United States case (lead CIT case for IEEPA refunds) has entered a critical phase regarding **finally liquidated entries** — those liquidated beyond the 90-day statutory reliquidation window (i.e., entries that are past the 180-day protest deadline tracked as `filingMethod: "litigation"` in the calculator).

Timeline:
- **April 17, 2026:** CIT issued a broad injunction ordering CBP to reliquidate entries in three buckets: (1) unliquidated; (2) liquidated but not final; and **(3) finally liquidated** — this third bucket is the controversial part.
- **Government position:** CBP has no authority to reliquidate "finally liquidated" entries without importer-specific court orders. The universal injunction "exceeds the Court's jurisdiction and equitable authority under *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*"
- **May 27, 2026:** Judge Eaton ordered CBP to explain its position on refunds for all entries regardless of liquidation status, with particular concern for informal entries where liquidation was simultaneous with entry.
- **June 6, 2026 (today):** Government deadline to appeal the injunction to the Federal Circuit. The Trump administration has signaled it will appeal. DOJ signaled intent; CAFC review is imminent.

**Calculator impact analysis:**

The `checkEligibility()` function in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts:262` currently categorizes entries as `filingMethod: "litigation"` when `daysRemaining < 0` (past the 180-day protest deadline). If the CIT's injunction for finally liquidated entries survives Federal Circuit review, this bucket could change from "litigation only" to a new COPE-style administrative path.

**Why no code change today:** The situation is highly fluid. The government is appealing on the day of this monitor run, and a Federal Circuit stay is possible. Changing `checkEligibility()` before the legal landscape settles would introduce incorrect guidance. The current `"litigation"` label accurately describes the *currently available* path even if the injunction expands options in the future.

**Recommended action when to update code:**
- If the Federal Circuit affirms the CIT injunction (no stay or stay lifted) → add a `"cape_finally_liquidated"` filing method and a new eligibility branch for entries with `liquidationDate` older than 180 days but still within the scope of a standing court order.
- If the Federal Circuit stays or reverses → no change needed; `"litigation"` label remains correct.
