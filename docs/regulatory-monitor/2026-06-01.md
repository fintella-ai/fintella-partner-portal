### 2. ⚠️ SIGNIFICANT: Section 122 Tariffs Ruled Unlawful — CIT (May 7, 2026) + CAFC Stay (May 12, 2026)

This is the most significant development since the April 29 seed file was generated.

**Timeline:**
- **May 7, 2026**: A divided CIT panel ruled that the Trump administration's 10% global Section 122 tariff (9903.03.01) is unlawful, holding that Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 does not authorize the tariff as imposed.
  - Sources: [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/), [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs), [BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/u-s-court-of-international-trade-invalidates-section-122-tariffs), [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/us-tariff-update-section-122-duties-found-unauthorized-by-law-ieepa-refunds-under-way)
- **May 8, 2026**: DOJ filed notice of appeal with U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC).
- **May 11, 2026**: DOJ filed emergency motion for stay pending appeal.
- **May 12, 2026**: CAFC entered an **administrative stay**, suspending the CIT order while the appeal is briefed.
  - Source: [Ward and Smith](https://www.wardandsmith.com/article/court-of-international-trade-rejects-10-section-122-tariff-what-businesses-should-know-while-the-appeal-proceeds), [Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/federal-circuit-hits-pause-on-cits-section-122-tariff-ruling/)
- **May 20, 2026**: CIT denied government's motion for stay pending appeal. Court found government failed to demonstrate irreparable harm, likelihood of success on the merits, or that public interest favored a stay. CAFC's own administrative stay remains in place regardless.
  - Source: [Customs & International Trade Law Blog](https://customsandinternationaltradelaw.com/2026/05/14/appeals-court-keeps-section-122-tariffs-in-place-for-now-what-importers-need-to-know/)

**Current status (June 1, 2026):**
- Section 122 tariffs (9903.03.01) **are still being collected** from non-party importers
- The CAFC administrative stay is in place pending appeal
- Section 122 tariffs are still scheduled to expire July 24, 2026 (150-day limit per statute)
- Relief is currently **limited to plaintiffs** in the CIT case; CBP continues collecting from everyone else
- If CAFC upholds the CIT ruling, Section 122 duties paid Feb 24 – Jul 24, 2026 may become separately refundable

**Impact on IEEPA refund calculator:**
- **NO change to `checkEligibility()` needed.** The `IEEPA_END_DATE = new Date("2026-02-23T23:59:59Z")` already correctly excludes all Section 122 entries (which began Feb 24, 2026). The date check at step 1 of `checkEligibility()` rejects them as `excluded_date`.
- **NO new rate entry needed.** The rate did not change (still 10% as `S122-01`).
- **Data update made**: The `S122-01.notes` field in `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json` has been updated to document the CIT ruling, the CAFC stay, and the current collection status, so anyone reading the seed data understands the legal posture.

**Watch**: If CAFC rules before July 24, 2026 (the Section 122 expiration date), or if Congress extends the 150-day window, a new monitor run should assess whether a Section 122 refund path should be added to the calculator.
