## Finding 1 — ACTIONABLE: CAPE Phase 2 Launched June 29, 2026

**Source**: CBP CSMS #68340863 update; cbp.gov IEEPA Duty Refunds page; SmarTrade/Thompson Hines June 2026 advisory

### What changed

CBP deployed CAPE Phase 2 within ACE on **June 29, 2026**. Phase 2 expands eligibility to:

- **Entry types 01, 02, 06** that are **flagged for reconciliation** where the reconciliation entry (type 09) **has not yet been filed**

Under Phase 1 (launched April 20, 2026), these entries were rejected by CBP's ACE system even though they are regular consumption entries. Phase 2 removes that restriction.

**Still excluded from CAPE (all phases)**:
- Type 09 entries themselves (the reconciliation filing)
- Type 47 drawback entries
- Type 08 (quota/visa)
- Type 23 (informal)
- Type 09 recon entries already filed prior to the CAPE declaration

**Not yet covered** (anticipated in future phases per CBP):
- Drawback entries (type 47)
- Reconciliation entries where type 09 has already been filed
- Entries under administrative protest
- Entries not filed in ACE

### Code changes made

**`src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`**:
1. Added `"cape_phase2"` to `FilingMethod` union type
2. Added `isFlaggedForReconciliation?: boolean` field to `EntryForEligibility`
3. Added `CAPE_PHASE2_LAUNCH_DATE` constant (June 29, 2026)
4. Updated `checkEligibility()` — step 4a: reconciliation-flagged entries route to `cape_phase2` (or `protest` if liquidated 80-180 days ago, or `litigation` if >180 days)
5. Updated `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` comment from "Phase 1" to "all phases"
6. Updated step 4 reason string from "excluded from CAPE Phase 1" to "excluded from CAPE"

## Finding 3 — MONITOR ONLY: Government Appeal of "Universal" Refund Order

**Source**: Foley & Lardner Jun 2026; Subject to Inquiry Jun 2026; PwC Canada tax insights

### What happened

CIT Judge Eaton issued an amended order on **March 27, 2026** covering all importers — including those who did NOT file suit — and ordering CBP to reliquidate ALL entries without IEEPA duties (even historically liquidated entries).

The government appealed, arguing:
1. CBP cannot reliquidate final liquidations absent statutory authority
2. The injunction was overbroad — should cover only named plaintiffs
3. Billions of dollars of IEEPA duties collected may not need to be refunded

**Current status as of July 5, 2026**: The appeal is pending in the Federal Circuit. The CAPE process (CBP-administered) continues on its own track regardless of this litigation. The appeal affects whether importers who did NOT file a CAPE declaration or protest can still claim refunds.

> **No code change required** — CAPE eligibility logic is unaffected. The dispute is over who gets refunds, not how the calculator determines eligibility. Watch for a Federal Circuit ruling that could expand or restrict the universe of eligible claimants.
