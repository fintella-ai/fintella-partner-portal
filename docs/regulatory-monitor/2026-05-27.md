## Findings

### 1. CIT May 7, 2026: Section 122 Tariffs Ruled Unlawful (ACTIONABLE — notes updated)

**What happened:** On May 7, 2026, the Court of International Trade (CIT) issued a 2-1 split opinion in *New American Economy Research Fund v. Trump* (Ct. No. 26-00080) holding that President Trump's invocation of Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 to impose a 10% global surcharge was unlawful. The majority held that the statutory prerequisite — "a large and serious United States balance-of-payments deficit" — was not satisfied, rendering the proclamation invalid.

**Relief granted:** Permanent injunction against CBP collecting Section 122 duties, but limited to the **three named importer plaintiffs only**. All other importers remain obligated to pay.

**Government appeal:** The Trump Administration appealed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC) on May 8, 2026.

**CAFC administrative stay:** On May 12, 2026, the CAFC issued an administrative stay suspending the CIT's order while the CAFC considers the government's motion for a stay pending appeal. Section 122 duties continue to be collected by CBP from all importers.

**CIT denied separate stay motion:** On May 20, 2026, the CIT denied the government's separate motion for a stay of enforcement pending appeal. The CAFC's own administrative stay controls.

**Current status as of 2026-05-27:** Section 122 10% global surcharge is still being collected by CBP from all importers. The tariff is still scheduled to expire July 24, 2026 (150-day statutory maximum). Congressional extension is considered unlikely.

**Impact on calculator:** No rate change. No eligibility change. Updated `S122-01.notes` in `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json` to document the legal challenge and current CAFC stay status.
