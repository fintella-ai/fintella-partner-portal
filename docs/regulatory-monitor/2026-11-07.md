## 1. Section 122 tariff expired July 24, 2026 — seed data gap

**Status: ACTIONABLE — fixed in this PR**

The Section 122 global surcharge (HTS 9903.03.01, 10%) expired at 12:01 AM EDT July 24, 2026, as
expected (maximum 150-day statutory window). The existing `S122-01` seed entry had `supersededBy: null`
and the notes did not reflect what replaced it.

**What replaced Section 122:** Section 301 (Trade Act of 1974 §301) replacement tariffs at 10% or
12.5% on imports from 60 economies took effect July 24, 2026 (91 FR 47318). Same Annex II exclusions
apply (USMCA goods, Section 232 products, critical minerals, pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, certain
electronics). These are forward-looking import tariffs, NOT refundable via CAPE.

**CIT unlawfulness ruling (additional context):** The Court of International Trade ruled the Section
122 surcharge unlawful on May 7, 2026 (appeal pending at the Federal Circuit as of November 2026).
This creates a potential refund claim for Section 122 duties paid Feb 24 – Jul 23, 2026, separate from
the IEEPA CAPE process. CAPE does NOT cover Section 122 refunds — those would require a protest or
CIT litigation on independent grounds. Added to seed data notes.

## 2. CAPE Phase 2 (June 29, 2026) and Phase 3 (October 6, 2026) — eligibility logic gap

**Status: ACTIONABLE — fixed in this PR**

CBP launched CAPE Phase 2 on June 29, 2026 and CAPE Phase 3 on October 6, 2026. The eligibility
code in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts` only knew about Phase 1.

**Phase 2 scope:** Reconciliation-flagged entries (types 01, 02, 06) where the Type 09 reconciliation
entry has NOT yet been filed. File CAPE declaration first; Type 09 may be filed after. Entries where
Type 09 is already on file remain excluded (future phase).

**Phase 3 scope:** Finally-liquidated entries (> 80 days since liquidation) for CIT plaintiffs only.
Requires the importer of record to have registered with CBP by July 30, 2026. CBP confirmed October 6
launch. Approximately $11.4B in finally-liquidated IEEPA duties eligible for this subset of importers.
CIT Judge Eaton ordered reliquidation of these entries on July 17, 2026. DOJ appealed the non-litigant
portion; Phase 3 is restricted to named plaintiffs.

**Code changes:** `FilingMethod` type updated to add `cape_phase2` and `cape_phase3`. `checkEligibility`
updated to route finally-liquidated entries that are within Phase 3 potential scope to
`cape_phase3` (with a `needsReview` flag since plaintiff status cannot be determined automatically).
A reconciliation-flagged note added for Type 09 exclusion.

## 3. China Section 301 exclusions expiring November 10, 2026 — WATCH (no code change)

**Status: WATCH — no calculator code change, John's review needed**

178 China Section 301 tariff exclusions (HTS 9903.88.69 and 9903.88.70) expire at 11:59 PM EDT
November 9, 2026 (effective November 10). As of late October 2026, USTR has not announced an
extension. After expiry, products currently entering free of Section 301 duties under these headings
revert to their standard Section 301 list rates.

**Why this matters for the calculator:** The existing `hasSection301` flag in `EntryForEligibility`
handles mixed-entry review notes. No IEEPA calculator logic change is needed. However, importers of
Chinese goods entering AFTER November 10 will face higher total tariff exposure, and clients may
need to understand their current Section 301 duty baseline.

**Action needed from John:** Monitor USTR for a last-minute extension announcement on Nov 8–10. No
PR change required unless an extension or modification is announced.
