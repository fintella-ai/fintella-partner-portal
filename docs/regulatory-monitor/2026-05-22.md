## 1. ⚠️ CIT Strikes Down Section 122 Tariffs (May 7, 2026)

**Status: ACTIVE LITIGATION — tariff still being collected (Federal Circuit stay)**

### What happened

On **May 7, 2026**, the U.S. Court of International Trade (CIT) issued Slip Op. 26-47 in a consolidated 2-1 ruling across two cases:

- *State of Oregon v. United States*, Court No. 26-01472
- *Burlap and Barrel, Inc. v. United States*, Court No. 26-01606

**Holding:** Presidential Proclamation 11012 (which imposed the 10% Section 122 global surcharge effective Feb 24, 2026) was **invalid** because it failed to identify the type of "balance-of-payments deficits" required by the statute. The court adopted the plaintiffs' argument that balance-of-payments deficits as defined by Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 cannot arise under a floating exchange rate system (which the U.S. has used since 1974).

**Injunction scope:** The CIT declined to issue nationwide relief. The permanent injunction was limited to three named importer plaintiffs:
1. Burlap and Barrel, Inc.
2. Basic Fun, Inc.
3. State of Washington (in its capacity as an importer)

### Federal Circuit stay (May 12, 2026)

On **May 12, 2026**, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit granted a **temporary administrative stay** of the CIT's permanent injunction while it considers the government's motion for a longer-lasting stay pending appeal. An expedited briefing schedule was set.

**Practical effect:** The 10% Section 122 tariff **continues to be collected from all importers** — including the three named plaintiffs — while the Federal Circuit appeal proceeds.
