## Findings

### 1. CAPE Phase 2 — LAUNCHED June 29, 2026 ⚠️ ACTION TAKEN

**Source:** CBP CSMS #69066837; Thompson Hine SmarTrade (https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/06/cbp-confirms-june-29-2026-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-phase-2-launch/); Diaz Trade Law (https://diaztradelaw.com/cape-ieepa-duty-refunds/)

CAPE Phase 2 opened June 29, 2026, expanding CAPE eligibility beyond Phase 1. The two Phase 2 categories are:

- **Reconciliation-flagged entries** (Types 01, 02, 06): Entries flagged for reconciliation where the Type 09 reconciliation entry has not yet been filed are now eligible, limited to unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation.
- **AD/CVD entries with DOC liquidation instructions**: Unliquidated AD/CVD entries for which the Department of Commerce has issued liquidation instructions are now eligible via Phase 2. Previously, all unliquidated AD/CVD entries were fully excluded.

**Impact on calculator:** The `checkEligibility()` function previously excluded ALL unliquidated AD/CVD entries. Phase 2 creates a new eligibility path for the subset with DOC instructions.

**What changed in code:**
- Added `"cape_phase2"` to the `FilingMethod` union type
- Added `hasDocLiqInstructions?: boolean` field to `EntryForEligibility` interface
- Updated the AD/CVD check (step 5) to route entries with DOC instructions to `filingMethod: "cape_phase2"`
- Added `cape_phase2: 5` to `FILING_RISK` map in `tariff-risk-score.ts`
- Added test for the new Phase 2 AD/CVD path
- Updated `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` comment to clarify Phase 2 context

**Note on Phase 3:** Law firm sources mention a Phase 3 for ~4,000 plaintiff importers who filed refund lawsuits — they receive refunds regardless of liquidation status. Phase 3 timeline/CBP implementation is not yet announced publicly. No code changes warranted until CBP issues CSMS guidance.

### 2. Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs — Effective July 24, 2026 ℹ️ DATA NOTE ONLY

**Source:** USTR (https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations); Honigman (https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462); C.H. Robinson (https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2026q3/07-24-26-client-advisory-new-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-now-in-effect/)

Section 122 tariffs expired July 24, 2026 (150-day statutory limit from Feb 24, 2026). On the same date, new Section 301 forced-labor tariffs took effect on ~60 trading partners:
- **10%** rate: Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, UK, Trinidad and Tobago, and others
- **12.5%** rate: remaining ~43 economies
- Applies to HTS Chapters 1–97
- Exemptions: civil aircraft, pharmaceuticals, aluminum/steel/copper, vehicles, wood products, semiconductors, humanitarian donations, informational materials

**Impact on calculator:** These tariffs are under a **different legal authority** (Section 301, not IEEPA or Section 122) and are **NOT refundable via CAPE**. The calculator handles IEEPA/Section 122 refunds only. No eligibility or rate logic changes needed.

**What changed in data:** Updated `S122-01` in `ieepa-tariff-rates.json` to set `supersededBy: "S301-FORCED-LABOR-01"` and added detailed notes about the CIT May 7 ruling, CAFC stay, and the Section 301 replacement context.

### 3. CIT Section 122 Ruling — May 7, 2026 (resolved by expiry) ℹ️ No action

**Source:** Skadden (https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs); Gibson Dunn (https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/)

CIT held Proclamation 11012 (Section 122 tariffs) invalid on May 7, 2026 — the proclamation failed to identify the specific type of balance-of-payments deficit required by 19 USC 2132(a). CAFC stayed the order May 12, 2026 while appeal proceeds. Moot because the tariffs expired naturally July 24, 2026.

No impact on IEEPA refund calculator — the IEEPA/Section 122 duties already paid remain refundable through CAPE regardless of this litigation.
