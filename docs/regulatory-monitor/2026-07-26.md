## Finding 1: Section 122 Expired July 24, 2026

**Source**: Multiple trade law publishers; USTR announcement July 23, 2026  
**URLs**:
- https://statt.com/blog/section-122-tariff-expiration-what-businesses-need-to-know/
- https://www.industrialsage.com/section-122-tariff-expires-july-2026/

**What happened**: The 150-day maximum term for Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. 2132) expired at 12:01 AM ET on July 24, 2026. The 10% global surcharge imposed by Presidential Proclamation on February 23, 2026 (effective February 24) was in effect for exactly 150 days — the statutory maximum.

**Impact on calculator**: The `S122-01` rate entry was already correctly dated (`endDate: "2026-07-23"`). Updated `supersededBy` to reference the Section 301 replacement. No change to refund eligibility logic — Section 122 duties paid through July 23 remain refundable via CAPE.

**Code change**: Updated `S122-01.supersededBy` from `null` to `"S301-FL-10PCT"` and updated notes to clarify the expiration.

## Finding 2: New Section 301 "Forced Labor" Tariffs — Effective July 24, 2026

**Source**: USTR Final Action announced July 23, 2026; CBP CSMS #69326983  
**URLs**:
- https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations
- https://globalimportblog.bakermckenzie.com/2026/07/24/united-states-new-10-to-12-5-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-on-over-60-countries-take-effect-july-24-2026-replacing-current-10-section-122-duties/
- https://content.govdelivery.com/bulletins/gd/USDHSCBP-421d887 (CBP CSMS #69326983)

**What happened**: USTR finalized Section 301 tariff actions under 60 forced-labor investigations (dockets USTR-2026-0265 and USTR-2026-0266). Duties took effect immediately as Section 122 expired.

**Key details**:
- Legal authority: Section 301 of Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. 2411) — NOT IEEPA
- **No statutory expiration** (unlike Section 122's 150-day cap)
- HTS Chapter 99 codes: 9903.05.20 through 9903.06.21
- Coverage: 60 economies, 99.4% of all U.S. imports

**Rates**:
| Rate | Countries |
|------|-----------|
| **10%** | Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad and Tobago, United Kingdom (17 economies) |
| **12.5%** | China, EU, Vietnam, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland, Brazil, Russia, Taiwan, and ~35 others (net-of-MFN for EU/Japan/South Korea/Switzerland/Taiwan) |

**Exemptions** (same broad scope as Section 122):
- USMCA-compliant goods from Canada (9903.05.93) and Mexico (9903.05.94) — exempt
- CAFTA-DR textiles and apparel
- Section 232 articles
- Civil aircraft
- Pharmaceutical articles
- Informational materials (Berman Amendment)
- Humanitarian donations

**CRITICAL CALCULATOR NOTE**: Section 301 duties are **NOT refundable via CAPE**. Only IEEPA duties (Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026) and Section 122 duties (Feb 24, 2026 – Jul 23, 2026) are recoverable. These new Section 301 entries are added to the rate database for reference/audit trail only.

**Code changes**:
- Added `S301-FL-10PCT` rate entry to `ieepa-tariff-rates.json`
- Added `S301-FL-125PCT` rate entry to `ieepa-tariff-rates.json`
- Added `section301ForcedLaborCodes` to `htsCodeReference` section
- Updated `_metadata.sources` with new citations

## Finding 3: CAPE Warehouse Entries (Types 21/22) Excluded — Effective July 7, 2026

**Source**: CBP CSMS #69127837  
**URLs**:
- https://content.govdelivery.com/bulletins/gd/USDHSCBP-41ece9d (CSMS #69127837)
- https://sobelnet.com/cbp-issues-critical-cape-update-warehouse-entry-directives-and-ieepa-refund-procedures/

**What happened**: Effective July 7, 2026, CBP excluded warehouse entries (Entry Types 21 and 22) from CAPE declarations. Any warehouse entry submitted after July 6 will receive an "ENTRY TYPE NOT ALLOWED" error. This reverses their earlier Phase 1 inclusion.

**Filing redirect**: Importers who paid IEEPA duties on warehouse goods must now file CAPE on the **warehouse withdrawals** (Entry Types 31, 32, 34, 38) instead. Withdrawals remain eligible.

**Retroactive concern**: Any Type 21/22 entries accepted on a CAPE Declaration between April 20 and July 6, 2026 without corresponding warehouse withdrawal CAPE filings will NOT receive reliquidation refunds. Affected filers need a separate CAPE Declaration covering the withdrawals.

**Code changes**:
- Added `"21"` and `"22"` to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` set in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`
- Added warehouse-specific reason text for eligibility exclusion
- Moved Types 21/22 from `included` to `excluded` in `capePhase1.eligibleEntryTypes` in `ieepa-tariff-rates.json`
- Added Type 31/32/34/38 redirect note

## Finding 4: CAPE Phase 3 — "Finally Liquidated" Entries (No Code Change)

**Source**: CIT Order July 17, 2026 (Judge Richard Eaton); CBP June 9 CIT hearing  
**URLs**:
- https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/07/cit-orders-cbp-to-process-ieepa-tariff-refunds-for-phase-3-finally-liquidated-entries/
- https://www.greenworldwide.com/cape-phase-3-for-ieepa-tariff-refunds-on-track-for-end-of-july-as-federal-circuit-appeal-continues/
- https://customsandinternationaltradelaw.com/2026/07/16/ieepa-cape-phase-three-refunds/

**What happened**: 
- CIT Senior Judge Richard Eaton issued an order July 17, 2026 directing CBP to reliquidate (without IEEPA duties) entries liquidated **more than 80 days ago** for plaintiffs with active CIT cases.
- CAPE Phase 3 technical capability is on track for end of July 2026.
- Government's position: Phase 3 refunds are only for importers who have filed lawsuits at the CIT (~3,700 active cases transferred to Judge Eaton's docket).
- CBP required to report Phase 3 progress by August 4, 2026.
- The government is appealing the broader CIT refund order at the Federal Circuit (separate from Phase 3 mechanics).

**Why no code change**: The calculator already correctly routes entries liquidated > 180 days as `filingMethod: "litigation"` with reason "Protest window expired — CIT litigation only." This is accurate: Phase 3 CAPE access requires an active CIT lawsuit, so the "legal required" routing in the calculator is correct. No `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` changes — Types 09, 47, 23, 21, 22 remain excluded from Phase 3 as well.

**Watch for**: If Phase 3 expands to importers WITHOUT active CIT suits (i.e., CBP processes automatically), the `checkEligibility()` logic would need updating. Monitor the August 4 CBP progress report.
