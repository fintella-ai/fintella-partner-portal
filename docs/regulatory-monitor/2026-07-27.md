## 1. CAPE Warehouse Entry Exclusion (Code Change — APPLIED)

**Source**: CBP CSMS #69127837  
**Effective**: July 7, 2026  
**Change applied**: Added entry types `21` and `22` to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`

CBP issued CSMS #69127837 effective July 7, 2026, clarifying that warehouse entries (CBP entry types 21 and 22) are not eligible for CAPE Phase 1 refund processing. The rationale: IEEPA duties were paid at the time of warehouse withdrawal (types 31, 32, 34, 38), not at warehouse entry. Any warehouse entry (type 21/22) accepted on a CAPE Declaration between April 20 and July 6, 2026, will **not** receive a refund — filers must resubmit covering the withdrawal entries instead.

**Prior code**: `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES = new Set(["08", "09", "23", "47"])`  
**Updated code**: `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES = new Set(["08", "09", "21", "22", "23", "47"])`

**Impact**: Calculator was incorrectly classifying warehouse entries (types 21/22) as potentially eligible. They are now correctly excluded.

## 2. Section 122 Expiration + Section 301 Replacement (Data Change — APPLIED)

**Sources**:  
- Statutory expiration: 19 U.S.C. § 2132 (150-day limit)  
- Section 301 final action: USTR July 24, 2026  
- Honigman alert: https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462

**What happened**: The Section 122 global 10% surcharge expired by operation of law at 12:01 AM ET on July 24, 2026 — the end of the 150-day statutory maximum. Simultaneously, USTR's Section 301 forced-labor investigation final action took effect.

**Section 301 key facts**:
- **Rate**: 10% or 12.5% ad valorem depending on country
- **Coverage**: ~60 economies (~99.4% of US imports by value)
- **HTS codes**: 9903.05.20–9903.05.84 (country-specific); 9903.05.85–9903.06.21 (exemptions)
- **Exemptions**: USMCA/CAFTA-DR goods, pharmaceuticals, civil aircraft, Chapter 98 provisions
- **Expiration**: None (unlike Section 122's statutory 150-day limit)
- **Applies to**: Goods entered on/after 12:01 AM ET July 24, 2026

**Important**: Section 301 tariffs are **not refundable under CAPE**. CAPE applies exclusively to IEEPA duties paid February 2025 – February 2026. Section 301 is a new prospective tariff regime, not a basis for IEEPA refund claims.

**Data changes applied**:
- `S122-01.supersededBy`: `null` → `"S301-FORCED-LABOR-01"`
- `S122-01.notes`: Added expiration date, CIT/Fed.Cir. litigation history, Section 301 successor note
- Added new entry `S301-FORCED-LABOR-01` documenting the Section 301 tariff (with disclaimer that it's context-only, not an IEEPA refund basis)
- Updated `_metadata.generatedDate`, `description`, `legalAuthority`, and `sources`

## 3. CIT CAPE Phase 3 Order — Monitoring Only (No Code Change)

**Source**: https://www.internationaltradeinsights.com/2026/07/cit-issues-order-with-specific-updates-related-to-cape-phase-3-finally-liquidated-entry-eligibility-for-ieepa-refunds/  
**Date**: CIT order July 15, 2026; reliquidation order July 17, 2026 (Senior Judge Richard Eaton)

The CIT ordered CBP to reliquidate finally-liquidated entries (liquidated >80 days ago) for importers who have filed suit at the CIT. This is CAPE Phase 3, expected to launch in late July 2026.

**Key constraint**: Phase 3 relief is available **only to CIT litigants**, not to all importers. Non-litigants with entries liquidated >80 days ago still have no automated refund path.

**No code change needed**: The calculator's existing `"litigation"` routing for entries >180 days past liquidation is correct — these entries require CIT suit to access Phase 3 Phase 3. The `"protest"` routing for 80–180 day entries is also unchanged.

**Watch for**: Phase 3 CAPE launch announcement (expected late July/early August 2026) that may expand to non-litigants — would require adding a new `"cape_phase3"` filing method.
