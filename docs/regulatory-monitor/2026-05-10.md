## 1. CIT Ruling: Section 122 Tariffs Declared Unlawful (ACTIONABLE)

**Source:** U.S. Court of International Trade — Slip Op. 26-47  
**URL:** https://www.cit.uscourts.gov/sites/cit/files/26-47.pdf  
**Date:** May 7, 2026  
**Coverage:** Holland & Knight (https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/us-court-of-international-trade-invalidates-the-administrations), Snell & Wilmer (https://www.swlaw.com/publication/more-tariff-refunds-not-so-fast-court-of-international-trade-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs-but-relief-is-limited-to-plaintiffs/), Norton Rose Fulbright (https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/199bf568/cit-rules-section-122-tariffs-invalid-limited-injunction-and-likely-appeal), Baker McKenzie (https://globalimportblog.bakermckenzie.com/2026/05/08/us-cit-invalidates-section-122-tariffs/), Customs & International Trade Law Blog (https://customsandinternationaltradelaw.com/2026/05/08/cit-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs-what-this-means-for-businesses/), JD Supra (https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/the-court-of-international-trade-rules-1176235/)

### What the court ruled

A 2-1 panel of the CIT held that Proclamation 11012 — which imposed a 10% global import surcharge under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 — is **ultra vires and unlawful**. The court found that the goods trade deficits cited by the government do not constitute "balance-of-payments deficits" as Congress understood the term when it enacted Section 122 in 1974. Because the proclamation lacked a valid statutory predicate, the tariffs cannot stand.

### Cases consolidated

| Plaintiff | Role |
|---|---|
| State of Oregon (lead), plus 22 other states | Dismissed for lack of standing |
| **State of Washington** | Permanent injunction granted |
| **Burlap and Barrel, Inc.** | Permanent injunction granted |
| **Basic Fun!, Inc.** | Permanent injunction granted |

### Scope of injunction

The injunction is **limited to the three named plaintiffs** above. It is **not a nationwide injunction**. All other importers must continue paying Section 122 duties under HTS 9903.03.01 unless and until the Federal Circuit or Supreme Court issues broader relief or a stay.

### Expected next steps

- Government is expected to appeal to the **U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit**
- Government may seek a **stay pending appeal** — if granted, collection would resume even for the three plaintiffs
- Federal Circuit may address: (a) merits of the Section 122 authority question, (b) whether nationwide relief is appropriate
- Further review by the Supreme Court is possible
- Timeline: Federal Circuit briefing and argument likely Q3–Q4 2026

### Impact on TIE calculator

**None.** Section 122 tariffs (effective Feb 24, 2026 onward, HTS 9903.03.01) fall **outside the IEEPA period** covered by CAPE. The `checkEligibility()` function's `IEEPA_END_DATE` of 2026-02-23 already excludes all Section 122 entries. No changes to eligibility logic are required.
