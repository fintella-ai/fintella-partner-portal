## 4. CIT Docket — Section 122 Tariff Ruling ⚠️ WATCH ITEM

**This is the major development this week. No calculator change required today, but active monitoring needed.**

### CIT ruling — May 7, 2026

The U.S. Court of International Trade issued a 2–1 decision in two consolidated cases holding that President Trump's invocation of Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. 2132) to impose the 10% global "balance of payments" surcharge (Proclamation 11012, Feb 23, 2026) was **unlawful**.

| Case | Docket |
|---|---|
| Oregon v. Trump | 26-cv-1472, US CIT |
| Burlap and Barrel, Inc. v. Trump | 26-cv-1606, US CIT |

**Holding:** The President did not meet the statutory criteria under Section 122 (which requires a fundamental disequilibrium in the U.S. balance of payments). The 2-1 majority found the criteria were not satisfied.

**Relief granted:** Permanent injunction limited to named plaintiffs only:
- Burlap and Barrel, Inc.
- Basic Fun, Inc.
- State of Washington

Nationwide injunction was **denied**. The court found broader relief was not warranted.

### CAFC administrative stay — May 12, 2026

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit issued an administrative stay of the CIT's injunction on May 12, 2026, pending the CAFC's consideration of the government's motion for a more durable stay pending appeal.

- Importers (other than the named plaintiffs) continue paying the 10% Section 122 tariff
- Fast briefing schedule set; plaintiffs' opposition brief due approximately May 19, 2026
- A ruling on the durable stay motion is expected within days to weeks of the opposition brief

Sources:
- https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/
- https://www.troutman.com/insights/federal-circuit-hits-pause-on-cits-section-122-tariff-ruling/
- https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/05/13/appeals-court-halts-order-finding-new-trump-tariffs-unlawful-for-now/183281/

### Calculator impact assessment

**No changes required today.** Three reasons:

1. **CAFC stay** — The Section 122 tariff is still being collected at 10% for virtually all importers. The rate in `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json` (entry `S122-GLOBAL-01`, rate 0.10, effectiveDate 2026-02-24, endDate 2026-07-23) correctly reflects the current legal reality.

2. **CAPE scope** — CAPE only covers IEEPA duties collected Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026. Section 122 duties (Feb 24, 2026 onward) are out of CAPE scope entirely, regardless of their legal validity. The calculator's date range check (`entry.entryDate < IEEPA_START_DATE || entry.entryDate > IEEPA_END_DATE`) at `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts:205` already excludes Section 122 entries.

3. **Uncertainty** — Even if the CAFC denies a durable stay, only named plaintiffs get relief. Any broader refund mechanism for Section 122 duties would require a separate government program analogous to CAPE, which has not been proposed.

**What to watch:** If the CAFC denies a durable stay AND the injunction expands (either by a new CIT order or Supreme Court cert), the Section 122 rate entry may need an `endDate` update and the eligibility date range would need no change (IEEPA period already ends Feb 23, 2026). Flag if the CAFC ruling is adverse to the government.
