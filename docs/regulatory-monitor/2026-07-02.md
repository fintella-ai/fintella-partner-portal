## 1. IRS Q3 2026 Overpayment Interest Rate: 6% → 7% (ACTIONABLE — applied)

**Source:** Federal Register 2026-13298, published July 1, 2026  
URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/01/2026-13298/quarterly-irs-interest-rates-used-in-calculating-interest-on-overdue-accounts-and-refunds-of-customs

**What changed:** The IRS overpayment interest rate rose from 6% (Q2 2026) to 7% effective July 1, 2026, for the calendar quarter July 1 – September 30, 2026. The federal short-term rate for April 2026 was 4%, driving Q3 back to 7% (same as Q1 2026 and all of 2025). Corporate rate is 6%.

**Impact on calculator:** The `calculateInterest()` function in `tariff-calculator.ts` uses quarterly rates loaded from the `InterestRate` DB table via the seed file. Q3 2026 was missing from `irsInterestRates.quarters` in `ieepa-tariff-rates.json`. Without this entry, interest calculations for entries with refund dates landing in Q3 2026 would cap at the Q2 rate. **Added Q3 2026 entry to seed JSON** — will be upserted on next Vercel build.

**Code change:** `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json` — added Q3 2026 quarter.

## 2. CAPE June 29 Update: Reconciliation-Flagged Entries Now Eligible (ACTIONABLE — applied)

**Source:** CBP CSMS #69066837 — "DEPLOYED – Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE) for IEEPA Refunds - Entries Flagged for Reconciliation"  
URL: https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds  
Effective: June 29, 2026

**What changed:** CAPE Phase 1 now accepts base entries (types 01, 02, 06) that are flagged in ACE for reconciliation, provided the reconciliation entry (Type 09) has **not** yet been filed. Once the Type 09 reconciliation entry is filed, the underlying base entries become **ineligible** for CAPE in this phase.

This is a two-sided rule:
- Base entry flagged for reconciliation + Type 09 not filed → **eligible** (CAPE processes IEEPA duties first; importer may then file Type 09 without the IEEPA duties included)
- Base entry flagged for reconciliation + Type 09 already filed → **ineligible** via CAPE

**Impact on calculator:** The `checkEligibility()` function did not previously track reconciliation status. Added two optional fields to `EntryForEligibility` (`isFlaggedForReconciliation`, `reconciliationFiled`) and added an exclusion check for the case where Type 09 has been filed. Entries where `reconciliationFiled` is unset or `false` continue to pass through normally (existing behavior preserved).

**Code changes:**
- `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts` — `EntryForEligibility` interface + new exclusion check (step 2a)

## 3. S122-01 Notes Updated: CIT Ruling + CAFC Stay + Section 301 Replacement (applied)

**Source:** Multiple — see below  
**What changed:** Several significant Section 122 developments since the last monitor run:

### CIT Slip Op. 26-47 (May 7, 2026) — Section 122 Tariffs Held Unlawful
The US Court of International Trade ruled that the Trump administration's 10% global Section 122 surcharge is unlawful. The court issued a limited injunction covering the State of Washington and two private importers. The CIT held that the Administration's interpretation of "fundamental international payment problems" under Section 122 was overly expansive.

Sources:
- https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/us-court-of-international-trade-invalidates-the-administrations
- https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs
- https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/u-s-court-of-international-trade-invalidates-section-122-tariffs
- https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/

### Federal Circuit Stay (June 11, 2026) — Duties Continue to Be Collected
The US Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC) stayed the CIT injunction, finding the government is "likely to succeed on the merits." CBP continues to collect Section 122 duties. Government brief due in July 2026. Appeal is unlikely to be resolved before the statutory July 24 expiration.

Source: https://www.troutman.com/insights/federal-circuit-hits-pause-on-cits-section-122-tariff-ruling/

### July 24, 2026 Expiration (22 days) — Hard Statutory Deadline
Section 122 has a hard 150-day statutory limit (19 U.S.C. §2132). It expires July 24, 2026 unless Congress acts to extend it. Congressional extension is considered unlikely.

### Section 301 Replacement Tariffs Proposed
USTR announced June 2, 2026 findings in 60 Section 301 investigations relating to forced labor. Proposed rates: **10%** for countries with a forced-labor import prohibition; **12.5%** for all others. Written comments due **July 6, 2026**; hearings **July 7, 2026**. USTR is targeting finalization before July 24.

Source: https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/june/ustr-makes-findings-and-proposes-action-60-section-301-investigations-relating-failures-take-action

**Note:** If Section 301 tariffs are formally imposed before July 24, a new rate entry will need to be added to `ieepa-tariff-rates.json`. Section 301 tariffs would be imposed under separate authority and would NOT be IEEPA tariffs — they would not affect the refund calculator's existing rate database, but would affect forward-looking import cost calculations. Monitor for a formal USTR determination and presidential proclamation.

**Code change:** `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json` — updated `S122-01.notes` with litigation status and replacement tariff outlook.
