## Non-IEEPA developments (informational only — no code changes required)

### Section 338 tariffs on Canada
- **August 19, 2026:** President signed three proclamations imposing **50% additional ad valorem duty** on Canadian goods under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930.
- **September 15, 2026:** CBP modified which Canadian-origin products are subject to the Section 338 tariff — 122 classifications added, some removed. Section 232 exemption narrowed to dairy list only.
- **Source:** [Greenberg Traurig trade update](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/7/us-trade-update-ieepa-refund-litigation-trade-fraud-enforcement--section-232-expansion), [CustomsGenius tariff codes 2026](https://www.customsgenius.com/tariff-codes-2026)
- **Calculator impact:** None — Section 338 is a separate authority from IEEPA. The refund calculator covers IEEPA tariffs (Feb 2025 – Feb 2026) and Section 122 (Feb 24 – Jul 23, 2026) only.

### Section 301 Forced-Labor tariffs (Section 122 replacement)
- **July 24, 2026:** USTR announced new Section 301 forced-labor tariffs of **10% or 12.5% on imports from 60 economies**, effective 12:01 AM July 24, 2026 (immediately after Section 122 expired).
- **Source:** [Honigman alert](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462)
- **Calculator impact:** None — these are not IEEPA tariffs and are not refundable via CAPE.

### CAPE Phase 2 real-world failures
- As of August 21, 2026: 272,029 CAPE declarations submitted; 191,494 passed validation; ~18.76M entries liquidated/reliquidated; ~$132.5B in potential refunds accepted for processing.
- As of July 3, 2026: 4.36M entries had been rejected in Phase 2 (reported at CIT July 15, 2026 status conference). CIT directed reliquidation of certain finally-liquidated entries ahead of a Phase 3.
- **Source:** [CBP IEEPA Duty Refunds page](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds)
