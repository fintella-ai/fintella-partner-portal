## Detail: Section 122 Expiry & Section 301 Forced Labor Tariffs

### What changed
Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 imposes a maximum 150-day tariff. The February 24, 2026 proclamation set the clock; July 24, 2026 was day 150. Section 122 expired at 12:01 AM ET July 24, 2026.

### Replacement tariffs (effective July 24, 2026)
The Administration replaced Section 122 with new tariffs under **Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. 2411)**, finalizing investigations into 60 economies' failure to prohibit imports produced with forced labor (investigations initiated March 12, 2026).

| Rate | Countries | HTS codes |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| 10% additional (on top of MFN) | ~57 economies including Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad & Tobago, United Kingdom | 9903.05.20–9903.05.84 (country-specific) |
| 10% all-in (includes MFN) | European Union, Taiwan | 9903.05.XX |
| 12.5% all-in (includes MFN) | Japan, South Korea, Switzerland | 9903.05.XX |

**Canada carve-out:** USMCA-compliant Canadian goods exempt (HTS 9903.05.93).  
**Product exemptions:** Civil aircraft (9903.05.88), pharmaceuticals (9903.05.89), in-transit goods entered before July 28 (9903.05.85), additional product-specific exclusions (9903.06.01–9903.06.21).

**Key difference from Section 122:** No statutory expiration date. Removal requires litigation, negotiation, or new administrative action.

### Impact on calculator
**None to IEEPA refund calculations.** The `checkEligibility()` function correctly gates on `IEEPA_START_DATE` / `IEEPA_END_DATE` (Feb 1, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026). Section 301 forced labor tariffs are a new collection regime, not IEEPA duties, and are not refundable under the CAPE/SCOTUS framework.  
**Action taken:** Updated `S122-01.supersededBy` from `null` to `"S301-FL-BASELINE-10"`. Added two new rate entries (`S301-FL-BASELINE-10`, `S301-FL-125-ALLIN`) as reference data. Added `section301ForcedLaborCodes` to `htsCodeReference`.

## Detail: CAPE Phase 2 (launched June 29, 2026)

### What changed
CBP launched CAPE Phase 2 on June 29, 2026, expanding the system to accept **entries flagged for reconciliation** (types 01, 02, 06) where the subsequent reconciliation entry (Type 09) has **not yet been filed**.

Phase 2 is limited to unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation.

### Impact on calculator
`EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` in `checkEligibility()` includes `"09"` (Reconciliation Summary). Phase 2 does **not** change this — the Type 09 summary itself remains excluded. Phase 2 opens eligibility for the underlying *flagged* entries (types 01, 02, 06) that were already in `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` only via the separate drawback exclusion, not via type code. These types 01/02/06 entries were already eligible in Phase 1.  
**No code change required.** Documented in `capePhase2` seed section.

Source: https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/06/cbp-confirms-june-29-2026-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-phase-2-launch/

## Detail: CAPE Phase 3 (CIT Order July 17, 2026)

### What changed
On July 17, 2026, CIT Senior Judge Richard Eaton issued an order directing CBP to **reliquidate finally-liquidated entries** — those where IEEPA duties were paid but entries were liquidated more than 80 days ago — for plaintiffs in the ~3,700 active IEEPA court cases before the CIT.

CBP was required to file a progress report with the court by 5:00 PM EDT on August 4, 2026.

Phase 3 targets late July / August 2026 deployment. This is separate from the ACE/CAPE automated system — reliquidation happens via court order.

### Impact on calculator
The `checkEligibility()` function already routes entries liquidated >180 days ago to `filingMethod: "litigation"`, which is the correct path for Phase 3 litigants. No code change is needed.

**Note for John:** Phase 3 is only available to importers with **active CIT cases**. The calculator could potentially flag this in the `reviewNote` field for entries routing to `filingMethod: "litigation"`, letting users know Phase 3 reliquidation may be available if they file at CIT. This would be a UX enhancement, not a logic change — deferred to your judgment.

Source: https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/07/cit-orders-cbp-to-process-ieepa-tariff-refunds-for-phase-3-finally-liquidated-entries/
