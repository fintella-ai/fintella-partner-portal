### 1. CIT Strikes Down Section 122 Tariffs — CAFC Stay in Effect

**Date**: May 7, 2026 (ruling); May 12, 2026 (CAFC stay); June 2, 2026 (appeal filed)  
**Sources**:
- Skadden: https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs
- Gibson Dunn: https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/
- Ward & Smith: https://www.wardandsmith.com/article/court-of-international-trade-rejects-10-section-122-tariff-what-businesses-should-know-while-the-appeal-proceeds

**Summary**: The U.S. Court of International Trade issued a 2-1 decision on May 7, 2026, holding that the 10% Section 122 global surcharge was unlawful because the statutory conditions required to invoke Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 were not met. The government appealed to the CAFC on May 8, 2026. The CAFC entered an administrative stay on May 12, 2026, suspending the CIT injunction while the appeal proceeds. **The 10% Section 122 tariff (HTS 9903.03.01) is still being collected as of today.** The July 24, 2026 statutory expiration is unchanged.

**Impact on calculator**: The S122-01 rate (10%, endDate 2026-07-23) is unchanged. Notes updated to reflect the contested legal status.

**Action taken**: Updated `S122-01.notes` in `ieepa-tariff-rates.json`.

### 2. CBP Reversal on Finally-Liquidated Entry Refunds (June 2, 2026)

**Date**: June 2, 2026  
**Sources**:
- National Law Review / Sheppard Mullin: https://natlawreview.com/article/cape-cope-cbp-reverses-position-universal-ieepa-duty-refunds
- Steptoe: https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/global-trade-and-investment-law-blog/trump-administration-appeals-latest-developments-in-ieepa-refund-process.html
- Davis Wright Tremaine: https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/06/appeal-of-trade-court-ieepa-duty-refund-order

**Summary**: On June 2, 2026, the Trump administration appealed the CIT's order (Atmus Filtration / Euro-Notions, Judge Eaton, April 7, 2026) that directed CBP to reliquidate "finally liquidated" entries — those where the 180-day protest window has closed (180+ days past liquidation). CBP now asserts it lacks statutory authority to refund finally-liquidated entries without an importer-specific court judgment. CAPE continues processing all unliquidated and non-finally-liquidated entries (approximately $85B refunded to date). CAFC appeal on finally-liquidated entries is pending.

**Impact on calculator**: This **confirms** the existing `excluded_expired → filingMethod: "litigation"` routing in `checkEligibility()` is correct. Entries liquidated more than 180 days ago cannot be refunded via CAPE; they require individual CIT litigation. No code change needed.

**Action taken**: Updated `capePhase1.legalBasis` in `ieepa-tariff-rates.json` to reflect the correct April 7, 2026 CAPE order citation and the June 2 CBP appeal.
