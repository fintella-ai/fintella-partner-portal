## 1. Section 122 Superseded by Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs (Jul 24, 2026)

**Source:** [Honigman alert — Section 122 Expires, Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs Replace](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462) · [EY Tax News 2026-1607](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1607-ustr-finalizes-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-on-60-economies-additional-tariffs-of-10-percent-or-125-percent-take-effect-24-july-2026) · [2026 HTS Revision 13](https://htshero.com/blog/2026-hts-revision-13)

**What changed:**
- Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 USC 2132) has a **150-day statutory cap**. With no Congressional extension, the 10% global surcharge expired at the close of **July 23, 2026**.
- HTS headings 9903.03.01–9903.03.11 (Section 122) expired simultaneously.
- USTR finalized a **Section 301 forced-labor action** effective **12:01 AM ET July 24, 2026**, covering ~60 trading economies in two tiers:
  - **10% tier** (~17 economies): Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad & Tobago, United Kingdom. Canada's USMCA-compliant goods remain exempt.
  - **12.5% tier** (~43 economies): China, Vietnam, Brazil, Thailand, Australia, Singapore, Philippines, Israel, Saudi Arabia, and others. EU and Taiwan have a combined cap at 10%; Japan, South Korea, and Switzerland have a combined cap at 12.5%.
- New HTS headings: **9903.05.20–9903.05.84** (rates) with exemptions under other 9903.05 and 9903.06 ranges.
- Product exclusions: civil aircraft/engines/parts, pharmaceutical articles, and goods already subject to Section 232 duties (steel, aluminum, copper, autos, semiconductors).

**Impact on TIE calculator:**
- **NOT an IEEPA refund issue.** Section 301 tariffs are prospective from Jul 24, 2026 — entirely outside the IEEPA refund eligibility window (Feb 1, 2025–Feb 24, 2026). No `checkEligibility()` logic changes needed.
- **Data model update made:** `S122-01.supersededBy` updated from `null` → `"S301-FL-10"`. Two new seed entries added: `S301-FL-10` (10% tier) and `S301-FL-125` (12.5% tier), both `rateType: "section301"`.
- **John's decision needed:** Should the UI/API surface Section 301 rates for importers wondering what their current tariff burden is (prospective, not refundable)? If yes, `lookupCombinedRate()` and the `breakdown` interface need a `section301` bucket. Currently Section 301 entries are in the seed but not surfaced by the calculator.

**Legal risk flag:** CIT may invalidate the Section 301 forced-labor tariffs. Plaintiffs argue the investigation was predetermined to replace Section 122 after the SCOTUS IEEPA ruling. Litigation expected. [Troutman Pepper analysis](https://www.troutman.com/insights/forced-labor-meet-section-301-new-tariffs-target-60-of-americas-biggest-trading-partners/)

## 2. CIT Class Action Certified for IEEPA Refunds (Oct 8, 2026)

**Source:** [Holland & Knight — Class Certification](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/court-of-international-trade-orders-nationwide-tariff-refunds) · [Steptoe analysis](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/global-trade-and-investment-law-blog/relief-in-sight-cit-certifies-class-for-ieepa-tariff-refunds-for-certain-importers.html) · [Troutman Pepper — refund status](https://www.troutman.com/insights/getting-duties-back-where-recovery-stands-under-ieepa-section-122-and-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs/)

**What changed:**
- Judge Richard Eaton (CIT) certified a **class of importers** on **October 8, 2026** covering importers who:
  - Paid IEEPA tariffs between **February 1, 2025** and **February 19, 2026**, AND
  - Have NOT filed individual refund lawsuits.
- A status conference was scheduled for October 13, 2026 for parties to discuss next steps.
- Government continues to appeal the CIT's earlier universal refund orders; government's appellate argument relies on *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, 606 U.S. 831 (2025).
- CBP began paying refunds on finally-liquidated entries for importers who filed individual suits.
- Separate Federal Register notice (Oct 5, 2026): CBP published information collection notice for "Court-Ordered Refunds Under IEEPA Worksheet" — estimated $166B in IEEPA duties collected Feb 3, 2025–Feb 24, 2026 subject to refund.

**Impact on TIE calculator:**
- **No immediate code change required.** `checkEligibility()` already returns `filingMethod: "litigation"` for entries past the 180-day protest window, which correctly encompasses joining the class action.
- **John's decision needed:** Should `reason` text for `filingMethod: "litigation"` be updated to mention the class action path explicitly (e.g., "Protest window expired — CIT litigation or class action (certified Oct 8, 2026) required")? This would be a UX improvement, not a logic change.
- **Also consider:** If class certification triggers a new refund window or deadline for class members, the eligibility logic may need updating once the Oct 13 status conference details are public.

## 4. CAPE Phase 2 — Reconciliation Entries (Jun 29, 2026)

**Source:** [CBP CAPE July Trade Information Notice](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/26_0723_trade_information_notice_CAPE.pdf) · [CBP CAPE Refunds page](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds)

**What it is:** CAPE Phase 2 launched June 29, 2026. It accepts entries flagged for reconciliation (entry types 01, 02, 06) where the reconciliation entry (Type 09) has not yet been filed. Also added warehouse withdrawal entries (Types 31, 32, 34, 38).

**Impact on TIE calculator:**
- `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` remains correct: Type 09 (the reconciliation entry itself) is still rejected by CAPE; only the underlying entries flagged for reconciliation are now accepted.
- Types 31, 32, 34, 38 were never in `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES`, so they were already treated as eligible — no change needed.
- Type 47 (drawback) remains excluded.
- **No code changes required.**
