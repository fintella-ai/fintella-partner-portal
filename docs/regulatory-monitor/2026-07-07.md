## ACTIONABLE CHANGES MADE THIS RUN

### 1. CAPE Rejects Warehouse Entries (Types 21/22) — Effective TODAY July 7, 2026

**Source:** CBP CSMS #69127837 (issued ~July 2, 2026)

**What changed:** Effective July 7, 2026, CAPE will reject Warehouse Entries (Entry Type 21) and Re-warehouse Entries (Entry Type 22). These entry types were mistakenly accepted by CAPE Phase 1 between April 20 and July 6, 2026. IEEPA duties on warehoused goods are assessed at the time of withdrawal from the warehouse, not at the time the warehouse entry is filed — so Types 21/22 have $0 IEEPA duties. Filers must submit the **withdrawal entries** (Types 31, 32, 34, or 38) instead.

**Code change:** Added `"21"` and `"22"` to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`.

**Impact on calculator:** Warehouse entries are now routed to `excluded_type` / `filingMethod: "none"`. The UI will correctly tell users to file the corresponding withdrawal entry.

**Note on pre-July-7 entries:** Any warehouse entry accepted by CAPE before July 7 without a corresponding withdrawal submission will NOT receive a refund. CBP will not (re)liquidate them with an IEEPA refund. The calculator cannot flag this retroactively — it's a CBP administration issue.

### Section 122 Expires July 24, 2026 (17 days)

- **Statutory hard deadline.** Section 122 authorizes tariffs for max 150 days. The 150-day clock runs out July 24, 2026.
- **Congressional extension:** None pending. The "Reclaim Trade Powers Act" is moving in the opposite direction.
- **EU already out:** US-EU deal effective July 1, 2026 creates a 15% all-inclusive ceiling for EU-origin goods. EU goods no longer pay Section 122 and won't pay any new Section 301 layer.
- **What to watch:** On or before July 20, 2026, USTR completes Section 301 investigations. A presidential proclamation imposing Section 301 duties could be signed any time between now and July 24. When it issues, a new rate entry will be needed.

**Next-run action:** If a Section 301 proclamation is published, add a new entry with:

### CAPE Phase 2 — Launched June 29, 2026 (Already Live)

- CAPE now accepts **reconciliation-flagged entries** (Types 01, 02, 06) even when no Type 09 reconciliation entry has been filed yet.
- The underlying entries (Types 01/02/06) are not excluded in our calculator, so they already route to `cape_phase1` eligibility. No calculator change needed.
- **Critical filing-sequence trap:** Filers must submit the CAPE Declaration FIRST, then file the reconciliation (Type 09). Filing Type 09 first locks the underlying entries out of CAPE in this phase. The calculator cannot detect this — it's a user advisory issue for the UI.

### CAPE Phase 3 — Expected End of July 2026

- **Scope:** Finally liquidated entries that paid IEEPA tariffs.
- **Limitation:** Government argues Phase 3 refunds only for importers who filed individual CIT suits. DOJ claims CBP cannot reliquidate finally liquidated entries without a court order specific to that importer.
- **CIT response:** June 9, 2026 hearing confirmed Phase 3 technical capability on track.
- **Current eligibility logic:** Finally liquidated entries beyond 180 days → `excluded_expired` / `filingMethod: "litigation"`. This is correct until Phase 3 scope is legally settled.
- **When to update:** If courts order CBP to issue Phase 3 refunds universally (not just to CIT plaintiffs), change `filingMethod: "litigation"` for `excluded_expired` entries to `"cape_phase3"` and add the new `FilingMethod` type.

### Government Appeal of Judge Eaton's Universal Refund Order (Euro-Notions)

- The DOJ is appealing the CIT's order that all importers (not just CIT plaintiffs) are entitled to IEEPA refunds.
- The Federal Circuit is also the venue for the Section 122 appeal, creating a coordinated appeal posture.
- If the Federal Circuit reverses Eaton's order, some importers may need to file individual CIT suits to get refunds.
- **No code change needed now.** CAPE Phase 1 operations are proceeding. Monitor for Federal Circuit ruling.

### CIT Invalidity of Section 122 (Stayed)

- CIT ruled 2-1 on May 7, 2026 that Section 122 was unlawfully invoked (State of Washington et al. v. U.S.).
- Federal Circuit stayed the ruling June 11, 2026 — tariffs still being collected.
- If the Federal Circuit reverses the CIT, Section 122 tariffs will be retroactively valid and no refund eligibility would arise.
- If the Federal Circuit affirms (or the stay expires), a new CAPE-like mechanism for Section 122 refunds would be needed.
- **No code change needed now.** Section 122 entries (Feb 24–Jul 23, 2026) are not in scope for any current refund pathway.
