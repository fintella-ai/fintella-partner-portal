**What**: Signed June 1, published June 4. Further adjusts Section 232 tariffs on aluminum, steel, and copper. Effective June 8, 2026. Uses Chapter 99 subchapter III HTS codes (9903.80.xx range). ## Development 2 — CBP Reverses on Finally-Liquidated Entries; DOJ Appeals (June 2, 2026) ⚠️ WATCH

**Sources**: [Sheppard / NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/cape-cope-cbp-reverses-position-universal-ieepa-duty-refunds) | [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/06/appeal-of-trade-court-ieepa-duty-refund-order) | [Fortune (June 1)](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/trump-administration-plan-appeal-illegal-ieepa-tariff-universal-refunds/) | [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/global-trade-and-investment-law-blog/trump-administration-appeals-latest-developments-in-ieepa-refund-process.html)

**What**: On May 29, 2026, CBP filed a motion in the CIT asserting — for the first time since the SCOTUS ruling — that it **lacks legal authority** to issue IEEPA refunds on "finally liquidated" entries (i.e., entries whose liquidation became final more than 180 days ago) without an importer-specific court judgment. On June 2, the DOJ formally appealed to the CAFC, seeking a stay of CIT Judge Eaton's order that had directed CBP to reliquidate all such entries universally.

This contradicts CBP's prior public FAQ guidance, which had stated that finally-liquidated entries would be covered in upcoming CAPE phases. Total IEEPA tariff exposure involved: CBP is processing roughly $85B in refunds total; the contested finally-liquidated slice is the remainder not yet refunded.

**Impact on calculator**: **No code changes needed.** The calculator already correctly routes entries 180+ days post-liquidation to `status: "excluded_expired"` / `filingMethod: "litigation"`. CBP's new position actually *confirms* this routing — importers in this bucket need individual court judgments, not the CAPE administrative process. The current behavior is accurate. If CBP loses the appeal and finally-liquidated entries become CAPE-eligible, `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` and `PROTEST_WINDOW_DAYS` logic would need revisiting.

**Flag for John**: If the CAFC denies the government's stay request and finally-liquidated entries do become universally refundable via reliquidation, the `filingMethod: "litigation"` classification for entries 180+ days out may become misleading. Worth a follow-up monitor in ~30 days.

## Development 3 — CIT Strikes Down Section 122 Tariffs; CAFC Stay in Place (May 7 / May 12, 2026) ⚠️ WATCH

**Sources**: [NatLawReview/CIT ruling](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/199bf568/cit-rules-section-122-tariffs-invalid-limited-injunction-and-likely-appeal) | [CAFC Administrative Stay (FreightFigures)](https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/cafc-administrative-stay-section-122-may-12-2026) | [Ward & Smith — appeal proceedings](https://www.wardandsmith.com/article/court-of-international-trade-rejects-10-section-122-tariff-what-businesses-should-know-while-the-appeal-proceeds) | [Gibson Dunn analysis](https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/)

**Timeline**:
- **May 7**: CIT 2-1 opinion — Section 122 Proclamation 11012 exceeded Presidential authority under 19 U.S.C. § 2132. Permanent injunction issued against CBP collection — but **limited to the three named plaintiffs** (State of Washington, Burlap & Barrel, Inc., Basic Fun, Inc.).
- **May 8**: Government appealed to CAFC.
- **May 12**: CAFC granted administrative stay — CIT's permanent injunction suspended while CAFC considers a longer-lasting stay pending appeal.
- **May 20**: CIT denied government's own motion to stay its ruling — but the CAFC's administrative stay supersedes this.

**Current state**: Section 122 tariffs **continue to be collected** for all importers except the three named plaintiffs (whose injunction is also stayed pending appeal outcome). The CAFC appeal is proceeding.

**Impact on calculator**: None right now. The S122-01 rate entry (10%, `effectiveDate: 2026-02-24`, `endDate: 2026-07-23`) remains accurate. If the CAFC eventually vacates the stay and the injunction takes nationwide effect, Section 122 refundability becomes a new question — but that is speculative and premature.

**Section 122 rate confirmed**: 10%. The social media announcement of 15% on Feb 22 was **not formally proclaimed**. All court filings and CBP guidance confirm the operative rate is 10%. The seed data `rate: 0.1` for S122-01 is correct.
