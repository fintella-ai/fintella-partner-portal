### 1. IRS Q3 2026 Interest Rate Added (`ieepa-tariff-rates.json`)

**Source**: IRS Revenue Ruling 2026-22; confirmed by Tax Notes, Federal Register notice 2026-09871  
**Change**: Added `Q3 2026` quarter to `irsInterestRates.quarters`

| Field | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 (new) |
|---|---|---|
| Period | Apr 1 – Jun 30, 2026 | **Jul 1 – Sep 30, 2026** |
| overpaymentRate (non-corp) | 6% | **7%** |
| overpaymentRateCorporate | 5% | **6%** |
| overpaymentRateCorporateLarge | 3.5% | **4.5%** |
| underpaymentRate | 6% | **7%** |
| underpaymentRateLargeCorporate | 8% | **9%** |
| federalShortTermRate | 3% | **4%** |

The rate increased because the federal short-term rate for April 2026 was 4% (up from 3% in January 2026), adding 3 basis points to arrive at 7% per IRC 6621. Interest calculations for IEEPA refunds covering entries that span into Q3 2026 (e.g., long-running unliquidated entries) will now use the correct 7% compounding rate.

**Why this matters**: The calculator uses daily-compounding quarterly rates per 19 USC 1505(c). Without Q3 2026, interest estimates for entries extending past June 30 would silently stop accruing (no matching quarter record in the DB).

### 2. S122-01 Notes Updated — CIT Ruling + Section 122 Context

**Source**: Oregon v. United States & Burlap and Barrel, Inc. v. United States, Slip Op. 26-47 (Ct. Int'l Trade May 7, 2026, Nos. 26-01472 & 26-01606)

Updated the `notes` field of rate entry `S122-01` to document:

- **May 7, 2026**: CIT 3-judge panel (2-1) struck down Section 122 tariffs as ultra vires. Court held the "large and serious" balance-of-payments criteria were not met under the statutory definition in effect when Congress enacted § 122 in 1974. **Permanent injunction entered — but limited to 3 named importer plaintiffs only** (State of Washington, Burlap and Barrel, Basic Fun). All other importers still owe the 10% surcharge.
- **May 8, 2026**: Government appealed to the Federal Circuit. Tariffs remain in effect for all non-plaintiffs pending appeal resolution.
- **July 24, 2026 expiration**: The 150-day statutory maximum is unchanged. The tariff expires automatically on this date for everyone regardless of appeal outcome. Congress has not authorized an extension, and the president cannot extend unilaterally.
- **Replacement watch**: USTR June 2, 2026 proposed Section 301 forced-labor tariffs (10%–12.5%) covering 60 economies. Hearings July 7, 2026. If finalized before July 24, these would provide a successor tariff mechanism — but these are Section 301, NOT Section 122, and would not be refundable via CAPE (CAPE covers only IEEPA-period duties Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026).

**Calculator impact**: No eligibility logic change needed. S122-01 endDate "2026-07-23" is correct. Section 122 is a *forward* tariff (not part of the refundable IEEPA period), so this ruling has no effect on CAPE refund calculations.

### Government Appeal of Euro-Notions IEEPA Refund Order

**Source**: Euro-Notions Florida v. United States, CIT No. 25-595; DOJ filing May 29, 2026; appeal filed June 2, 2026 to the Federal Circuit

**Summary**: The government filed an appeal of Judge Eaton's universal IEEPA duty refund order (which directed CBP to reliquidate all unliquidated and recently-liquidated entries without IEEPA duties). The government's specific target is "finally liquidated" entries — entries where the 180-day protest window has **already closed** (liquidated more than 180 days ago). CBP argued it lacks authority to reliquidate these without importer-specific court judgments.

**Status**: DOJ sought a stay; stay status was not confirmed as of this run. CAPE processing continues normally for unliquidated and recently-liquidated entries. CBP reports: ~16 million entries covered by CAPE declarations, ~8.5 million reliquidated without IEEPA duties, $20.6 billion in refunds disbursed to Treasury.

**Calculator impact**: **No change needed.** The calculator already correctly routes entries by `filingMethod`:
- `cape_phase1`: Unliquidated OR liquidated ≤80 days ago → unaffected by appeal
- `protest`: Liquidated 80–180 days ago → still within the protest window (19 USC 1514); importers should file protests as a precaution given appeal uncertainty
- `litigation`: Liquidated >180 days ago → these are the "finally liquidated" entries the government is targeting; already routed correctly

The routing logic in `checkEligibility()` (`src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`) is accurate. The appeal may affect whether CBP will automatically process reliquidation for `protest`-bucket entries, but that is a CBP operational issue, not a calculator logic issue.

**Watch**: If the Federal Circuit grants a stay of the Euro-Notions order, update the `protest` bucket messaging to recommend urgency (file protests immediately regardless of CAPE timeline).
