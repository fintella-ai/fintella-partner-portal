### 1. Section 122 Tariffs — Legal Challenge (ACTIONABLE)

**Status: CONTESTED — still being collected under CAFC stay**

| Event | Date | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| CIT ruling | May 7, 2026 | 2-1 majority ruled Section 122 tariffs unlawful |
| CIT injunction | May 7, 2026 | Permanent injunction entered against collection from plaintiff-importers |
| CIT denies stay | May 8–9, 2026 | CIT denied government's motion to stay its own order |
| CAFC admin stay | May 12, 2026 | CAFC suspended CIT's injunction; CBP resumes full collection |
| Current status | May 28, 2026 | 10% surcharge still being collected from all importers |

**Case:** *State of Oregon v. United States*, No. 26-01472 (Ct. Int'l Trade, May 7, 2026)

**Holding:** The CIT majority (2-1) held that the statutory prerequisite for invoking Section 122 — a "large and serious United States balance-of-payments deficit" — was not satisfied. The President's proclamation of February 23, 2026 therefore exceeded his authority under 19 U.S.C. § 2132.

**Appeal:** The Trump administration appealed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC). On May 12, 2026, the CAFC issued an administrative stay while it considers the government's motion for a more durable stay pending full appeal briefing and argument.

**Effect on TIE calculator:**
- **Rate: unchanged** — 10% is still being collected via HTS 9903.03.01
- **Statutory expiry: unchanged** — July 23/24, 2026 (150-day limit under 19 U.S.C. § 2132)
- **Eligibility logic: no change** — Section 122 entries (Feb 24, 2026 onward) are not currently within CAPE Phase 1 scope
- **Seed data updated:** `S122-01.notes` in `ieepa-tariff-rates.json` updated to reflect legal status

**Watch for:**
- CAFC ruling on more durable stay (expected June–July 2026)
- If CAFC denies stay: CBP must stop collecting → Section 122 refund opportunity emerges, potentially requiring CAPE Phase 2 expansion or new refund mechanism
- If CAFC grants stay: 10% continues until statutory expiry (Jul 24) or CAFC merits decision
