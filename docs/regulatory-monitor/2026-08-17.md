## 2. Section 122 Expiration → Section 301 Replacement (CRITICAL)

### What Happened
The Section 122 10% global surcharge **expired July 24, 2026** (maximum 150-day statutory term). Within hours, USTR Jamieson Greer announced final action under Section 301 investigations into forced-labor trade practices, imposing new tariffs on **60 economies** covering 99.4% of U.S. imports.

### New Section 301 Rate Structure (Effective July 24, 2026)
| Tier | Rate | Countries | Criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partial compliance | **10%** | ~14 economies | Already ban or committed to banning forced-labor imports |
| No compliance | **12.5%** | ~46 economies | No forced-labor import prohibition in place |

**10% countries include**: Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, UK, Trinidad & Tobago

**12.5% countries**: China, EU member states, Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, Brazil, and ~40 others

**HTS codes**: 9903.05.20 through 9903.05.84 (country-specific headings)

**Key exemptions**: USMCA-compliant goods (Canada/Mexico), CAFTA-DR textiles, Section 232 articles, civil aircraft, pharmaceuticals, goods in-transit before July 24

**Critical distinction from Section 122**: Section 301 has **no statutory expiration date** and **no rate cap** (vs. Section 122's 150-day term and 15% cap). Liberty Justice Center has already challenged the Section 301 authority in court.

**Impact on calculator**: Section 301 tariffs (July 24, 2026 onward) are NOT IEEPA tariffs and are NOT refundable under CAPE. The IEEPA refund period ended Feb 23, 2026. Section 301 tariffs fall completely outside the calculator's refund scope. However, the seed data was updated to document S122-01's `supersededBy` linkage for reference completeness.

## 4. CBP CAPE System Updates

### CAPE Phase 2 — LAUNCHED June 29, 2026 ✅

**What changed**: CBP launched CAPE Phase 2 effective June 29, 2026. Phase 2 expands CAPE eligibility to include entries **flagged for reconciliation** (parent entry types 01, 02, 06) where the reconciliation entry (Type 09) has not yet been filed.

**Key conditions for Phase 2 eligibility**:
- The underlying entry must be unliquidated OR liquidated within 80 days of the Phase 2 filing date
- The formal Type 09 reconciliation entry must NOT have been filed yet
- File the CAPE Phase 2 declaration BEFORE filing the Type 09 reconciliation entry (order matters)

**Financial scale**: ~$28.7B in additional refunds, ~2.8M additional entries covered (per CBP's June 9, 2026 CIT hearing testimony)

**Calculator update**: Added `"cape_phase2"` to `FilingMethod` type and new `isFlaggedForReconciliation` field on `EntryForEligibility`. Reconciliation-flagged entries now route to `cape_phase2` instead of `cape_phase1`.

### CAPE Phase 3 — CIT Order July 17, 2026

**What changed**: CIT Senior Judge Richard Eaton issued an order on July 17, 2026 directing CBP to reliquidate finally-liquidated entries (liquidated >80 days ago, past the 180-day protest window) for importers who have filed individual suits at the CIT. The court will issue case-specific orders in approximately 3,700 individual IEEPA cases.

**Key limitations**:
- Phase 3 refunds apply ONLY to importers who have already filed CIT lawsuits
- The federal government is contesting the legal basis for finally-liquidated entry refunds
- "Non-litigant" importers (those who did not file suit) must file affirmative cases to access Phase 3 refunds
- As of July 31, 2026: 75,000+ CAPE declarations submitted; 17.69M entries processed; ~$128.68B in certified/potential refunds

**Calculator update**: Added `"cape_phase3"` to `FilingMethod` type. Entries past the 180-day protest window that are finally-liquidated now show `filingMethod: "litigation"` with updated reason text noting that CIT-ordered Phase 3 reliquidation may be available for importers who have filed suit.

## 6. IRS Interest Rate — Q3 2026 Change ✅

### What Changed
The IRS overpayment interest rate **increased from 6% (Q2 2026) to 7% (Q3 2026)**, effective July 1, 2026. This affects the interest calculation for IEEPA refunds where the deposit-to-refund period spans into Q3 2026.

| Quarter | Non-Corporate Rate | Corporate Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 2026 | 7% | 6% |
| Q2 2026 | 6% ↓ | 5% ↓ |
| Q3 2026 | 7% ↑ | 6% ↑ |

**Impact**: Entries whose interest calculation spans July 1, 2026 onward will now accrue at 7% (from 6% in Q2). Most entries in the IEEPA period would have received their refunds by now, but any still being processed (Phase 2, Phase 3) will benefit from the Q3 rate.
