## 1. CODE CHANGE APPLIED — Warehouse Entries Excluded from CAPE

**Source:** CSMS # 69127837 (CBP, effective July 7, 2026)
**URL:** https://content.govdelivery.com/bulletins/gd/USDHSCBP-41ece9d (CSMS bulletin; see also https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/cbp-cape-ieepa-refund-progress/)

**What changed:** CBP issued CSMS # 69127837 clarifying that warehouse entry types **21** and **22** are no longer accepted on CAPE Declarations, effective July 7, 2026. These entry types will be rejected with the existing "ENTRY TYPE NOT ALLOWED" error. This resolves an ambiguity from Phase 1 launch (April 20) where some warehouse entries were being submitted without corresponding withdrawal entries.

- **Excluded (effective July 7):** Entry Types 21 (warehouse entry) and 22 (re-warehousing)
- **Still eligible:** Entry Types 31, 32, 34, 38 (warehouse withdrawals) — IEEPA duties are paid at withdrawal, not at entry into warehouse

**Code updated:** `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts` — added `"21"` and `"22"` to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES`. Updated the user-facing exclusion reason to remove the "Phase 1" qualifier (now applies across CAPE phases). Comment updated with rationale.

**Implication for importers:** Any importer who submitted a Type 21/22 warehouse entry on a CAPE Declaration between April 20 and July 6 will NOT receive a refund via that filing. They must resubmit using the warehouse withdrawal entries (Types 31/32/34/38) on which IEEPA duties were actually paid.

**Source:** Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. § 2132); multiple trade counsel analyses
**URLs:**
- https://www.tarifftax.org/analysis/section-122-what-comes-next

**What's happening:** The 10% global Section 122 surcharge (9903.03.xx codes, effective February 24, 2026) automatically expires by operation of law at 12:01 AM EDT on July 24, 2026. The 150-day statutory maximum is absolute — Congress, not the President, would need to extend it. No extension legislation is pending.

**Seed data status:** `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json` entry `S122-01` has `endDate: "2026-07-23"` — **correct and no update needed.** The calculator already treats the Section 122 rate as expired after this date.

**Note on Federal Circuit stay:** The CIT struck down Section 122 tariffs in May 2026, but the Federal Circuit granted a stay, so the tariffs have continued to be collected. Moot on July 24 regardless.

**Action needed now:** None for the calculator. The expiration is already modeled. Watch for any last-minute action in the next 14 days.

## 3. WATCH — Section 301 Replacing Section 122 (Proposed, Not Yet Final)

**Source:** USTR Federal Register notice, June 2, 2026; public hearing July 7, 2026
**URLs:**
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/05/2026-11296/notice-of-determinations-and-request-for-comments-concerning-actions-in-section-301-investigations
- https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/june/ustr-makes-findings-and-proposes-action-60-section-301-investigations-relating-failures-take-action
- https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/section-301-replacing-section-122

**What was proposed:** On June 2, 2026, USTR proposed Section 301 tariffs on 60 economies (59 countries + EU) based on findings that they fail to impose and enforce import prohibitions on forced-labor goods. Rates: 10% for 15 trading partners, 12.5% for 45 trading partners. Public comment period closed July 6; hearing was July 7.

**Expected timeline:** USTR is expected to finalize around July 18-20, timed to take effect when Section 122 expires July 24. The HTS codes would be in Chapter 99 (new sub-headings TBD). Section 301 has no rate cap and no 150-day limit, unlike Section 122.

**Action needed now:** NONE — tariffs are not finalized. **Standby to add new seed data entries** once the final Federal Register notice is published. The new rates will be `rateType: "section301"` with country-specific entries. Expect HTS codes in the 9903.04.xx range or similar.

**This is the highest-priority upcoming data update.** If Section 301 finalizes before the next monitor run, the seed data will need new entries for each country tier.

## 4. WATCH — CAPE Phase 3 (Finally Liquidated Entries, Late July 2026)

**Source:** CIT hearing June 9, 2026; multiple trade law firm analyses
**URLs:**

- https://blog.tradewin.net/cape-refunds-what-importers-need-to-know-about-phase-2-3

**What's coming:** CBP testified at the June 9 CIT hearing that CAPE Phase 3 infrastructure will be ready in late July 2026. Phase 3 would cover finally liquidated entries (liquidated > 80 days ago, i.e., outside the current CAPE window).

**Critical caveat:** The DOJ continues to argue CBP lacks authority to reliquidate finally-liquidated entries without individual CIT lawsuits. The Federal Circuit is reviewing this. Current government position: Phase 3 refunds will flow only for importers who have filed protective actions at CIT. Non-litigants remain excluded.

**CAPE Phase 2 update (for reference):** Launched June 29, 2026 per CSMS # 69035485. Covers entries of Types 01, 02, 06 that are *flagged for reconciliation* but whose reconciliation entry (Type 09) has not yet been filed. Type 09 itself remains excluded. No change needed to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` for this.

**Code action needed:** None now. If Phase 3 launches and the government authorizes non-litigant refunds (i.e., drops the CIT-lawsuit requirement), the eligibility logic for `filingMethod: "litigation"` entries may need updating to reflect a new `filingMethod: "cape_phase3"` path. Monitor the Federal Circuit appeal.

**What happened:** The DOJ filed a notice of appeal to the Federal Circuit on June 2, challenging the CIT's order (in *Euro-Notions Florida v. United States*) requiring universal IEEPA refunds, specifically contesting the requirement to reliquidate entries that are already final. The Federal Circuit issued a stay pending appeal. The government argues the CIT's order constitutes an impermissible universal injunction.

**Impact on calculator:** None currently. The `checkEligibility()` function already correctly routes:
- Unliquidated / liquidated < 80 days → `cape_phase1`
- Liquidated 80-180 days → `protest`
- Liquidated > 180 days → `litigation`

The stay doesn't change these pathways for users of our calculator.
