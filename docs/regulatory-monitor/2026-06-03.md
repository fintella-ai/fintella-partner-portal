## 2. Section 122 Status — SIGNIFICANT LEGAL DEVELOPMENT (May 7, 2026)

**Source**: [Gibson Dunn analysis](https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/) | [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/us-court-of-international-trade-invalidates-the-administrations) | [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs) | [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/more-tariff-refunds-not-so-fast-court-of-international-trade-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs-but-relief-is-limited-to-plaintiffs/)

### What happened

On **May 7, 2026**, a three-judge CIT panel ruled **2-1** in *Burlap & Barrel, Inc. v. United States* that President Trump's Section 122 tariffs (Proclamation 11012) exceed the President's statutory authority. The majority held that Section 122 requires identification of "balance-of-payments deficits" under the specific historical metrics Congress contemplated in 1974 (liquidity/official settlements balances vis-à-vis foreign currencies), not modern current-account metrics. A permanent injunction was entered prohibiting collection of Section 122 duties — **but only for the three named plaintiffs**: Burlap & Barrel Inc., Basic Fun Inc., and the State of Washington.

On **May 12, 2026**, the Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC) entered an **administrative stay** suspending the CIT's order while the government's appeal proceeds. 

### Current status (as of Jun 3, 2026)

- **Section 122 tariff IS STILL BEING COLLECTED** by CBP for all importers, including the original plaintiffs, due to the CAFC stay.
- Relief is limited to the three named plaintiffs pending CAFC resolution.
- The tariff is on track to **expire naturally at 12:01 AM ET on July 24, 2026** (150-day statutory maximum from Feb 24, 2026).
- An extension beyond July 24 **requires Congressional legislation** — the President cannot restart the 150-day clock unilaterally (though informal discussions of alternative authority exist). No Congressional action has been announced as of this writing.

### Calculator impact

**No rate change.** Section 122 rate remains 10% (`9903.03.01`), collected through at least July 23, 2026. The `S122-01` seed record's `endDate` of `2026-07-23` remains correct.

**Notes field updated** in `S122-01` to reflect the CIT ruling, the CAFC stay, and the plaintiffs-only scope of current relief. The prior notes referenced "Trump announced intent to raise to 15%" — clarified that no formal 15% proclamation was ever issued.

## 3. CIT — Euro-Notions Florida v. United States — WATCH ITEM (May 27, 2026)

**Source**: [International Trade Insights](https://www.internationaltradeinsights.com/2026/05/cit-orders-customs-to-explain-its-position-on-issuing-ieepa-refunds-for-all-entries-regardless-of-liquidation-status/) | [Thompson Hines SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/06/doj-signals-intent-to-appeal-cits-ieepa-tariff-refund-injunction-after-cbp-commissioner-ordered-to-appear/) | [National Law Review/Sheppard](https://natlawreview.com/article/cape-cope-cbp-reverses-position-universal-ieepa-duty-refunds)

### What happened

On **May 27, 2026**, following a closed settlement conference in *Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. United States* (CIT No. 25-00595), Senior Judge Eaton issued two orders directing CBP to:

1. **Explain its legal position** on issuing IEEPA refunds for entries whose liquidation has become **finally liquidated beyond CBP's 90-day reliquidation authority** (19 U.S.C. §1501).
2. Address the particular problem of **millions of informal entries** (typically low-value) where liquidation occurred simultaneously with entry filing, making them final before CAPE Phase 1 ever launched.

The court expressed concern that CBP's current CAPE Phase 1 framework leaves these importers with no automated path and that the government has indicated it will only refund duties on historically liquidated entries **if the importer files a CIT complaint**.

Additionally, a separate order (*V.O.S. Selections* case) reportedly directs CBP to refund IEEPA duties for **all importers** who paid, including those with final liquidations — not just lawsuit filers. DOJ has signaled intent to appeal this broader injunction.

### Upcoming proceedings

- **June 10, 2026**: CBP must file a progress report on CAPE with the CIT.
- **June 11, 2026**: Closed settlement conference.

### Calculator impact

**No code change warranted today.** The existing eligibility logic correctly handles historically liquidated entries:

- Entries with `liquidationDate` > 180 days ago → `status: "excluded_expired"`, `filingMethod: "litigation"` — this is the correct path because the only current avenue is filing a CIT complaint.
- If the June 11 conference produces a court order expanding eligibility (e.g., CBP ordered to issue refunds to all importers regardless of liquidation status), the `checkEligibility()` function will need a new filing method (e.g., `"cape_universal"`) and `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` may need adjustment.

**MONITOR AGAIN after June 11, 2026** for CIT outcome.
