### 2. ⚠️ CAPE Phase 2 (Reconciliation Entries) — Launching June 29, 2026 — ACTIONABLE (informational)

**Source**: CBP testimony at CIT hearing, June 9, 2026 (Euro-Notions Florida v. United States)  
**What it is**: CAPE Phase 2 extends the automated refund channel to cover entries flagged for reconciliation (Types 01, 02, 06) **where a Type 09 has not yet been filed**.  
**Key distinction**: The Type 09 entry itself remains excluded. Phase 2 lets the underlying entries get their IEEPA refund before the reconciliation is filed.  
**Liquidation window**: Same 80-day window as Phase 1 — unliquidated or liquidated within 80 days of CAPE submission.  
**Limitation**: Entries filed before approximately May 31, 2025 that liquidated on a standard 314-day cycle will have liquidated > 80 days before June 29, 2026 and fall outside Phase 2 scope.  
**CBP recommendation**: Hold Type 09 reconciliation filings (unless deadline is within 30 days) so the underlying entry can receive IEEPA refund via CAPE first.  
**Calculator impact**: No change to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` — Type 09 remains excluded. Updated the Type 09 exclusion reason string to explain this distinction to users. Added `capePhase2` documentation section to seed data. Updated comment in `tariff-calculator.ts`.

### 3. ⚠️ Section 122 Tariffs — CIT Invalidity Ruling + CAFC Stay — INFORMATIONAL

**Sources**:
- CIT ruling: *State of Oregon et al. v. Trump*, May 7, 2026 (2-1 divided opinion)
- Federal Circuit administrative stay: May 12, 2026
- Law firm coverage: Holland & Knight, Skadden, BDO, Cooley (May 2026)

**What happened**: The U.S. Court of International Trade held on May 7, 2026 that the Trump administration exceeded its statutory authority in invoking Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, finding the statutory conditions (fundamental international payments problem) were not met. CIT issued a permanent injunction — but only covering the three named importer plaintiffs: State of Washington, Burlap and Barrel, Inc., and Basic Fun, Inc.

**Government response**: Appeal filed May 8, 2026. The Federal Circuit issued an administrative stay of the CIT's order on May 12, 2026 pending consideration of a motion for a more durable stay pending appeal.

**Practical effect**: Section 122 tariffs (10%, HTS 9903.03.01) continue to be collected from all non-plaintiff importers. The CAFC stay means the narrow injunction is itself stayed — effectively reinstating full tariff collection even for the three plaintiffs during the appeal.

**Natural expiry**: Section 122 expires July 24, 2026 (150-day statutory limit). Congress can extend; President cannot do so unilaterally. As of June 21, 2026, no extension has been enacted.

**Calculator impact**: No rate change — `S122-01.rate` remains 0.10. No eligibility change — Section 122 tariffs are not IEEPA tariffs and not refundable through CAPE (the calculator's `IEEPA_START_DATE`/`IEEPA_END_DATE` bounds correctly exclude the Section 122 period from IEEPA refund eligibility). Updated `S122-01.notes` in seed data to document the ruling and stay.

**Watch**: If Section 122 expires July 24 without extension, no calculator change is needed (already has correct `endDate: "2026-07-23"`). If a new tariff authority replaces it, that would require a new rate entry.

### 4. ⚠️ CAPE Phase 3 (Finally Liquidated Entries) — Announced, Scope Contested — WATCH

**Source**: CBP testimony at June 9, 2026 CIT hearing in Euro-Notions Florida v. United States  
**What CBP said**: A CBP representative testified that CAPE Phase 3 will begin processing entries with finally liquidated status (protest window expired, liquidated > 80 days ago).  
**Contested scope**: The Trump administration simultaneously argues that Phase 3 eligibility is limited to importers who filed Section 1581(i) actions at the CIT — not all importers. This contradicts CBP's published FAQ page.  
**Separate government appeal**: The Trump administration is also appealing Judge Eaton's universal IEEPA refund order to the Federal Circuit, arguing the government can retain refunds for finally liquidated entries not subject to specific court orders.

**Calculator impact**: No change. The `filingMethod: "litigation"` routing for `excluded_expired` entries (protest window expired, > 180 days since liquidation) remains the correct conservative guidance. When CBP issues a formal CSMS message with binding eligibility rules for Phase 3, that would require updating `checkEligibility()` to potentially add a new `"cape_phase3"` filing method.

**Watch**: A formal CSMS announcement for Phase 3 should prompt an update to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` and `FilingMethod` type in the calculator.
