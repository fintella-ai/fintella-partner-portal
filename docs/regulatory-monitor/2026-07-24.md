## 2. CIT Struck Down Section 122 as Ultra Vires (May 7, 2026)

**What happened:** The U.S. Court of International Trade ruled on May 7, 2026 that Proclamation 11012 (Section 122) was ultra vires. The court found the proclamation nowhere identified a "balance-of-payments deficit" as Congress defined the term in 1974 (measured by liquidity, official settlements, and basic balance) — instead citing only trade deficits and current account deficits, which are distinct concepts. The court entered permanent injunctive relief for three named importer plaintiffs only; the tariff remained in effect for all other importers. The government appealed to the Federal Circuit on May 8, 2026.

**Sources:**

## 4. CAPE Warehouse Entry Change — Types 21 & 22 Now Rejected (CSMS #69127837, July 7, 2026)

**What happened:** CBP issued CSMS #69127837 effective July 7, 2026: warehouse entries (Entry Types 21 and 22) are no longer accepted on a CAPE Declaration. Submitting them now returns an "ENTRY TYPE NOT ALLOWED" error. Warehouse withdrawals (Types 31, 32, 34, 38) continue to be accepted, with refunds processed upon (re)liquidation of the associated warehouse entry. Warehouse entries accepted on CAPE between April 20 and July 6 without a corresponding withdrawal will NOT receive an IEEPA refund — filers need a separate CAPE Declaration covering the withdrawals.
