### 3. USTR Announcements — Section 301 Forced Labor Tariffs ⚠️ DATA CHANGE MADE

**Status: ENACTED — replaced Section 122 on July 24, 2026**

Section 122 tariffs hit their 150-day statutory cap at 12:01 AM ET on July 24, 2026 and expired. President Trump did not raise the rate to 15% (announced via social media Feb 22 but no proclamation issued). Congress did not act to extend.

**Immediately upon Section 122 expiry**, USTR's final Section 301 forced-labor tariff action (published July 23, 2026) took effect, covering 60 economies representing ~99.4% of U.S. imports:

| Tier | Rate | Qualifying Economies |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | **10%** | ~20 economies with an ART (Agreement on Reciprocal Trade) or existing forced-labor import ban (EU, UK, Japan, South Korea, Australia, and others) |
| Tier 2 | **12.5%** | ~40 remaining economies with no ART and no sufficient ban |

**HTS Codes:** `9903.05.20` through `9903.05.84` (individual economy codes); exemptions under `9903.05.85`–`9903.05.99` and `9903.06.01`–`9903.06.21`

**Key exemptions:**
- USMCA/CAFTA-DR qualifying goods
- Civil aircraft
- Pharmaceuticals (carve-out under `9903.04.60`–`9903.04.66`, effective July 31, 2026)
- ~471 additional HTS subheadings (expanded from proposed list after public comment)
- Goods in transit before July 24 — grace period through July 28, 2026

**Critical note for IEEPA calculator:** These are **Section 301 tariffs, not IEEPA tariffs**. They apply to goods entered **on or after July 24, 2026** — entirely outside the IEEPA period (Feb 1, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026). The `checkEligibility()` date range check (`IEEPA_START_DATE` / `IEEPA_END_DATE`) correctly excludes any post-IEEPA entry. No change to eligibility logic needed.
