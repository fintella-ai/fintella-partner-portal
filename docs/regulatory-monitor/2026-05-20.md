## 5. CIT Docket — Section 122 Tariff Ruling ⚠️ MATERIAL DEVELOPMENT

**Status: CIT struck down Section 122 tariffs May 7, 2026. Federal Circuit stay May 12. Collection continues.**

### Ruling (May 7, 2026) — Slip Op. 26-47
The U.S. Court of International Trade issued a **2-1 decision** holding that President Trump's invocation of Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 was unlawful, finding the statutory "balance of payments deficit" prerequisite could not be satisfied under the modern floating exchange rate system.

**Case names:**
- *State of Oregon, et al. v. United States, et al.* (state coalition plaintiffs)
- *Burlap and Barrel, Inc., et al. v. United States, et al.* (private importer plaintiffs)

**Injunction scope (NARROW):**
The CIT issued a **permanent injunction against collection from 3 specific plaintiffs only:**
1. Burlap & Barrel, Inc.
2. Basic Fun! Inc.
3. State of Washington

Nationwide injunctive relief was **denied**. Other state plaintiffs were dismissed for lack of standing (states that are not importers). All other importers continue to pay Section 122 duties.

### Federal Circuit Stay (May 12, 2026)
The government appealed immediately. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC) issued an **administrative stay** of the CIT's injunction on May 12, 2026, preserving the status quo while the CAFC considers a motion for a more durable stay pending appeal. Section 122 duties are being collected from all importers (including the 3 named plaintiffs, while the stay is in effect).

### Impact on the Tariff Refund Calculator

| Dimension | Impact |
|---|---|
| Rate (10%, `9903.03.01`) | **No change** — tariff still being collected under stay |
| `endDate` (`2026-07-23`) | **No change** — statutory 150-day cap still operative |
| IEEPA CAPE eligibility | **No change** — Section 122 entries are outside IEEPA refund scope |
| `S122-01` notes | **Updated** — legal challenge now documented |

### Monitoring priority — HIGH
The Federal Circuit could:
(a) Grant a permanent stay pending appeal → Section 122 collected through July 24 statutory expiration  
(b) Deny permanent stay → Collection halts for all importers (not just the 3 plaintiffs) if the injunction is broadened  
(c) Rule on the merits during appeal → Full reversal or affirmance

A SCOTUS cert petition is expected regardless of CAFC outcome, given the national importance. Importers paying Section 122 duties should be documenting entries for potential future refund claims.
