### 1. Section 301 Forced Labor Tariffs — Replaced Section 122 (July 24, 2026)

**Status**: Active  
**In database**: ❌ No (but arguably out of scope — see below)

The Section 122 10% surcharge expired at its statutory 150-day limit on July 24, 2026. Within hours, USTR implemented a new Section 301 tariff regime:

- **Authority**: Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 (not IEEPA, not Section 122)
- **Basis**: Failure of 60 economies to enforce forced labor import prohibitions
- **Rates**: 10% (countries with forced labor prohibition) or 12.5% (countries without)
- **Coverage**: 60 economies covering ~99.4% of U.S. imports
- **Effective**: July 24, 2026, 12:01 AM ET
- **HTS codes**: 9903.05.20 through 9903.05.84 (per country), exemptions at 9903.05.85–9903.06.21
- **Exemptions**: USMCA goods, civil aircraft, pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, autos, wood (U.S. Note 52 to Subchapter III, Chapter 99)
- **Federal Register**: FR 2026-15181 (published July 28, 2026)
- **USTR press release**: https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations
- **CBP CSMS**: CSMS #69326983 — Guidance: Section 301 Forced Labor Import Duties

**Why not in the IEEPA tariff rates database**: Section 301 tariffs are NOT IEEPA tariffs and NOT refundable via the CAPE mechanism. The calculator's purpose is IEEPA tariff refunds, so Section 301 is out of scope for `ieepa-tariff-rates.json`. The existing `hasSection301` flag in `EntryForEligibility` (tariff-calculator.ts:78) already handles the case where Section 301 and IEEPA duties were both paid on an entry, triggering a human review flag.

**Action needed**: None for the calculator's core logic. Consider whether the TIE's rate lookup UI needs a "current tariff" view that shows Section 301 rates — that would be a new feature, not a regulatory monitor fix.
