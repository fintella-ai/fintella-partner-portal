## Material Changes Found

### 1. Section 122 EXPIRED — Replaced by Section 301 (effective July 24, 2026)

**What happened**: The Section 122 global surcharge (10%) expired at 12:01 AM ET on July 24, 2026, as the 150-day statutory maximum under 19 U.S.C. 2132 elapsed. Congress did not vote to extend; the President has no authority to extend Section 122 beyond 150 days.

**Replacement**: USTR finalized a new tariff action under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 based on forced-labor investigations. Two-tier structure:
- **10% rate** — 17 economies that adopted a forced-labor import prohibition (Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad & Tobago, United Kingdom)
- **12.5% rate** — remaining ~43 economies (Algeria, Angola, Australia, Brazil, China, Egypt, Iraq, Israel, Japan, Kazakhstan, Libya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Norway, Philippines, Qatar, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Africa, Thailand, Türkiye, UAE, Uruguay, Venezuela, Vietnam, and others)
- HTS codes: 9903.05.20–9903.05.84 (rates), 9903.05.85–9903.06.21 (exemptions)

**Impact on calculator**: These are NOT refundable via CAPE. Only IEEPA tariffs (Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026) are refundable. The Section 301 rates are recorded in the seed data as reference context only.

**Legal status**: CIT challenge filed (In Re: Section 301 Forced Labor Cases, consolidated Aug 2026); oral argument Sep 30, 2026. No injunction issued. Tariffs currently in effect.

**Source**: https://www.internationaltradeinsights.com/2026/07/ustr-imposes-new-section-301-tariffs-targeting-forced-labor-concerns-across-60-economies/

### 2. CAPE Phase 2 Launched (June 29, 2026)

**What happened**: CBP launched CAPE Phase 2 on June 29, 2026, expanding IEEPA duty refund eligibility to entries flagged for reconciliation.

**New eligibility**: Entry types 01, 02, and 06 that have been flagged for reconciliation in ACE are now eligible, **provided** the corresponding type 09 reconciliation entry has NOT yet been filed. Same 80-day liquidation window applies.

**Estimated scale**: $28.7B in potential refunds across ~2.8 million reconciliation-flagged entries.

**Still excluded in Phase 2**: Type 47 (drawback). Future phase anticipated for drawback entries.

**Important caveat**: If the reconciliation filing deadline is within 30 days, CBP advises prioritizing the reconciliation filing over the CAPE declaration.

**Source**: https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds

### 3. IRS Overpayment Interest Rates — Q3 and Q4 2026 Added

- **Q3 2026 (Jul–Sep)**: 7% individual / 6% corporate (INCREASED from Q2's 6%/5%)
- **Q4 2026 (Oct–Dec)**: Unchanged from Q3 — 7% individual / 6% corporate

**Source**: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/interest-rates-remain-the-same-for-the-fourth-quarter-of-2026
