### 2. CIT Rules Section 122 Tariffs Unlawful; CAFC Stay Entered (May 7–12, 2026)

**Source:**  
- Gibson Dunn: https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/  
- Skadden: https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs  
- Thompson Coburn: https://www.thompsoncoburn.com/insights/77-may-7-2026-court-of-international-trade-determines-section-122-tariffs-are-unlawful/  
- BDO: https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/u-s-court-of-international-trade-invalidates-section-122-tariffs

**Type:** CIT ruling + CAFC administrative stay

On May 7, 2026, the Court of International Trade issued a 2-1 split opinion holding that the Trump Administration's 10% global Section 122 tariffs are unlawful. The court found that the president's proclamation did not satisfy the statutory "balance-of-payments deficit" criterion under 19 U.S.C. §2132 — the deficit must be measured by one of three specific accounting methods (liquidity, official settlements, or basic balance) that Congress contemplated when it enacted Section 122 in 1974, not by the trade deficit metric the Administration relied upon.

**Practical impact is limited:**
- The permanent injunction applies only to the three named plaintiff importers who brought the case.
- The vast majority of importers remain obligated to pay Section 122 duties.
- On May 12, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit entered an administrative stay, suspending the CIT's order while the government's appeal proceeds.

**Current status (June 10, 2026):** Section 122 tariffs are still being collected at 10% for essentially all importers. The CAFC appeal is pending. The 150-day statutory expiration (July 24, 2026) remains the operative end date.

**Impact on calculator:** No rate change. `S122-01` rate stays 0.10, endDate stays `2026-07-23`. Notes updated to reflect legal uncertainty. If CAFC overturns the stay and the injunction is broadened, a rate change PR would be required immediately.

**Watch:** If the CAFC issues a decision broadening the injunction before July 24, the `S122-01` `endDate` would need to be revised. Also watch for any Congressional action to extend Section 122 beyond July 24 (requires legislation, not a proclamation).

### 3. Government Appealed CIT Universal IEEPA Reliquidation Injunction (June 2, 2026)

**Source:**  
- Davis Wright Tremaine: https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/06/appeal-of-trade-court-ieepa-duty-refund-order  
- Thompson Hine SmarTrade: https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/06/trump-administration-appeals-cits-ieepa-tariff-refund-order/  
- Baker McKenzie: https://globalimportblog.bakermckenzie.com/2026/06/08/us-trump-administration-appeals-cits-ieepa-tariff-refund-injunction/  
- Steptoe: https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/global-trade-and-investment-law-blog/trump-administration-appeals-latest-developments-in-ieepa-refund-process.html

**Type:** CAFC appeal of CIT injunction

Background: After the February 20, 2026 SCOTUS ruling, the CIT issued a universal injunction on April 17, 2026 ordering CBP to refund IEEPA duties across three categories: (1) unliquidated entries, (2) liquidated entries where liquidation is not yet final, and (3) finally-liquidated entries.

The government accepts categories (1) and (2) — CBP is processing approximately $85 billion in IEEPA refunds through CAPE for unliquidated and non-final entries.

For category (3) — **"finally liquidated" entries** — the government appealed on June 2, 2026. The government's position:
- For entries where importers have filed CIT cases: reliquidation must come from importer-specific judicial orders, not a blanket injunction.
- For entries where importers have **not** filed suit: the CIT's universal injunction is unlawful under *Trump v. CASA, Inc.*, because it applies beyond the named parties (a non-party universal injunction).

**Impact on calculator eligibility logic:** No code change needed.  
- The `checkEligibility()` function correctly routes entries liquidated >180 days ago (expired protest window) to `filingMethod: "litigation"`. This remains accurate — these entries require CIT litigation, not CAPE.  
- The `litigation` routing is now more nuanced: importers who have not yet filed at the CIT face uncertainty on whether CBP will reliquidate without a specific court order. This is a legal complexity that warrants professional counsel — exactly what the `legal_required` routing bucket communicates.  
- No changes to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES`, `PROTEST_WINDOW_DAYS`, or `CAPE_PHASE1_LIQUIDATION_WINDOW_DAYS` constants.

**Watch:** If the CAFC rules against the universal injunction, it would confirm that "finally liquidated, non-litigant" entries cannot be refunded without filing a new CIT case. The calculator would need a note added to the UI for the `litigation` path. This is a UI/copy update, not a code logic change.
