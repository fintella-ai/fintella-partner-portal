## Key Findings (since last seed-data update, April 29, 2026)

### 1. CAPE Phase 2 Launched June 29, 2026 ✅ ACTIONABLE — IMPLEMENTED

**Source:** CBP CSMS #69035485 and #69066837; confirmed by multiple trade counsel advisories.

**What changed:** Phase 2 of the Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE) system went live in ACE on June 29, 2026. Phase 2 accepts entries **flagged for reconciliation** (entry types 01, 02, 06) where the corresponding reconciliation entry (Type 09) has not yet been filed.

- Same 80-day liquidation window as Phase 1
- CAPE removes IEEPA duties from the flagged entries *before* the Type 09 reconciliation entry is filed, separating the IEEPA refund from the reconciliation calculation
- Entries where a Type 09 is already on file are NOT eligible under Phase 2 (future phase)
- 1.6 million reconciliation-flagged entries filed in CAPE as of June 30

**Code change:** Added `cape_phase2` to `FilingMethod` type and `isReconciliationFlagged` flag to `EntryForEligibility` in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`. Reconciliation-flagged entries now route to `cape_phase2` instead of `cape_phase1`.

### 2. CAPE Phase 3 Confirmed for End of July 2026 ⚠️ WATCH — NOT YET LIVE

**Source:** CBP testimony at CIT hearing, June 9, 2026; multiple trade advisories.

**What changed:** CBP executive assistant commissioner Susan Thomas confirmed at a June 9 CIT hearing that Phase 3 will be technically ready by end of July 2026. Phase 3 covers **finally liquidated entries** (i.e., entries liquidated more than 80 days ago that are outside the Phase 1 window).

- Entries worth approximately $11.4 billion in disputed IEEPA duties
- **Critical caveat:** DOJ continues to argue CBP lacks authority to reliquidate finally liquidated entries without an individual CIT lawsuit (Federal Circuit No. 26-1898). Technical readiness ≠ refunds flowing
- Currently calculator routes these as `filingMethod: "litigation"` — correct until Phase 3 launches and legal dispute is resolved

**Code change:** None yet. Updated `FilingMethod` JSDoc comment to note "CAPE Phase 3 pending end of Jul 2026." Watch for CBP CSMS announcing Phase 3 deployment.

**Action needed when Phase 3 launches:** Update `PROTEST_WINDOW_DAYS` logic or add `cape_phase3` filing method for finally liquidated entries.

### 3. CIT Struck Down Section 122 Tariffs (May 7, 2026) — CAFC Stay in Place ⚠️ WATCH

**Source:** State of Oregon v. United States, Nos. 26-01472 & 26-01606, Slip Op. 26-47; Holland & Knight advisory; BDO advisory.

**What changed:**
- **May 7, 2026:** CIT issued 2-1 decision that Section 122 tariffs were unlawful — President's proclamation did not satisfy the statutory conditions of 19 U.S.C. §2132 (required: fundamental international payments problem; conditions not met per court)
- **May 12, 2026:** U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC) issued an administrative stay, suspending the CIT's injunction pending appeal (No. 26-1898)
- **Current status:** Section 122 tariffs are still being collected. The stay means importers cannot yet claim Section 122 refunds even if the CIT was right

**Calculator impact:** None to IEEPA refund eligibility. IEEPA dates/rates are unaffected. If the CAFC upholds the CIT ruling before July 24 expiration, Section 122 refunds would become available via a future CAPE extension or separate process — but that is speculative and not yet implemented.

**Seed data change:** Updated `S122-01.notes` to document the CIT ruling, CAFC stay, and case citations.

### 4. Section 122 Expires July 24, 2026 (11 Days) ⏰ WATCH

**Source:** 19 U.S.C. §2132 statutory 150-day cap; Proclamation 11012 (FR 2026-03824).

**What it means:**
- Section 122 **cannot** be extended by the President unilaterally — 150-day cap is statutory
- Congress has not enacted an extension (considered unlikely)
- If Section 301 tariffs are not in place by July 24, imports revert to MFN + existing Section 232 + existing China Section 301

**Calculator impact:** `S122-01.endDate = "2026-07-23"` is already correctly set in the seed data. No rate change needed; IEEPA refund calculator is unaffected (IEEPA period ended Feb 23, 2026).

### 5. USTR Section 301 Proposed Replacement — No Final Determination Yet ⏰ WATCH

**Source:** USTR Federal Register notice (June 5, 2026, document 2026-11296); USTR press release June 2, 2026; White & Case advisory.

**What's pending:**
- USTR launched 60 Section 301 investigations (forced labor basis) on March 11, 2026
- Proposed rates: 10% for economies with forced-labor import prohibitions; **12.5%** for all others
- 46 countries at 12.5% including China, Vietnam, India, Japan, South Korea, EU members, Brazil, Turkey, Thailand
- Public comment period closed July 6, 2026; public hearing held July 7, 2026
- Rebuttal comments due ~July 12, 2026
- USTR final determination expected before July 24 (to avoid tariff gap)
- **No final determination as of July 13, 2026**

**Calculator impact when finalized:** A new Section 301 tariff rate (not IEEPA) will apply post-July 24. The TIE calculator covers IEEPA refunds (entries from Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026), so Section 301 going forward does not affect *refund* eligibility. However, the rate database will need a new entry for reference.

**Action needed when finalized:** Add new rate entries to `ieepa-tariff-rates.json` with `rateType: "section301"` for each affected country, effective July 24, 2026 (or whenever proclamation states).
