### 2. Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs Now in Effect ✅ UPDATED

USTR Section 301 investigation (determination June 2, 2026; public hearing July 7, 2026) imposed new tariffs effective simultaneously with Section 122 expiration.

**Details**:
- **Authority**: Section 301, Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. 2411)
- **Effective**: July 24, 2026, 12:01 AM ET
- **Countries**: ~60 countries (covering ~99% of US imports)
- **Rates**: 10% (baseline — countries with qualifying forced-labor import prohibition) OR 12.5% (higher tier — countries without)
- **HTS Chapter 99 codes**: 9903.05.01–9903.05.09 (baseline tier); 9903.05.86–9903.05.87 range (higher tier)
- **No statutory expiration** (unlike Section 122's 150-day cap)
- **Carve-outs**: USMCA-qualifying CA/MX goods; Section 232 steel/aluminum; certain critical minerals, pharma, semiconductors
- **Entry rule**: Entries before July 24 owe Section 122; entries on or after July 24 owe Section 301

**⚠️ IMPORTANT**: These Section 301 tariffs are NOT IEEPA tariffs. They are NOT refund-eligible via CAPE. The TIE calculator should display them as informational for "current regime" context only.

**Note**: Per-country tier assignment (10% vs 12.5%) requires verification against the official USTR Federal Register notice — added as `S301-BASELINE` and `S301-HIGHER` with notes flagging this.

### 4. Warehouse Entry Types 21 and 22 Removed from CAPE ✅ UPDATED

**CBP CSMS #69127837** (effective July 7, 2026): Warehouse entries (Entry Types 21 and 22) are no longer accepted on CAPE Declarations. Any submission including them receives an "ENTRY TYPE NOT ALLOWED" error.

**Rationale**: IEEPA duties on warehoused goods are assessed at withdrawal time, not at initial warehouse entry. The correct vehicle is the **withdrawal entry** (Types 31, 32, 34, 38), which remains CAPE-eligible. Refunds are processed upon (re)liquidation of the associated warehouse entry.

**Fix**: Added `"21"` and `"22"` to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts` with explanatory comment.
