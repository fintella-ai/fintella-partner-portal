### 2. CIT Section 122 Ruling — SIGNIFICANT MONITORING ITEM

**⚠️ Major development — not yet actionable but requires ongoing monitoring.**

On **May 7, 2026**, the U.S. Court of International Trade issued a **2-1 decision** in *Oregon v. Trump / Burlap & Barrel, Inc. v. Trump* holding that the Administration's 10% global tariff imposed under **Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974** (19 U.S.C. § 2132) is **unlawful**.

Key facts:
- The CIT found the presidential proclamation exceeded the narrow authority delegated by Congress under Section 122, which requires specific "balance of payments" conditions not satisfied by general trade/current account deficits.
- A **permanent injunction** was issued — but **limited to the three prevailing plaintiffs only**: Burlap & Barrel, Inc., Basic Fun!, Inc., and the State of Washington.
- The CIT declined a nationwide injunction because most state plaintiffs lacked Article III standing (they were not importers of record and alleged only indirect economic harm).

### 3. CAFC Administrative Stay — Section 122 Still Being Collected

**On May 8, 2026**, the Government appealed the CIT ruling to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC).

**On May 12, 2026**, the **CAFC entered an administrative stay**, suspending enforcement of the CIT's permanent injunction while the court considers the government's motion for a full stay pending appeal.

**Current status as of May 17, 2026:**
- CBP is **continuing to assess and collect** the 10% Section 122 tariff on covered imports from all importers.
- The three named plaintiffs (Burlap & Barrel, Basic Fun!, State of Washington) may have temporary relief, but the administrative stay complicates even that.
- The CAFC has not yet ruled on the full stay pending appeal — that decision is pending.

**Calculator impact: None currently.** The `S122-01` rate record (10%, effective 2026-02-24, end 2026-07-23) accurately reflects the current collection status. No rate update is needed while the CAFC stay is in effect.

### 4. Section 122 July 24 Expiration — No Change

Section 122 tariffs **expire on July 24, 2026** under the statutory 150-day cap (19 U.S.C. § 2132(a)). The President **cannot extend Section 122 unilaterally** — congressional legislation would be required. No extension bill has been introduced.
