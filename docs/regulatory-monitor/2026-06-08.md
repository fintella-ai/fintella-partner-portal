USTR announced proposed Section 301 tariffs following investigations into 60 economies for failure to prohibit forced-labor goods:

- 10% ad valorem — 15 economies (including specific named partners)
- 12.5% ad valorem — 45 economies

Rates and country scope will likely differ from Section 122's universal 10%. If enacted before or at the July 24 expiration, new rate entries will be needed in `ieepa-tariff-rates.json` under `rateType: "section301"`.

### 4. Court of International Trade — Section 122 Ruling

**Material development: CIT ruled Section 122 unlawful on May 7, 2026. CAFC administrative stay keeps tariffs in force.**

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| May 7, 2026 | CIT panel issues divided opinion — Section 122 tariffs are unlawful; statutory conditions for invoking Section 122 were not met |
| May 7, 2026 | Permanent injunction issued — scoped only to 3 named plaintiffs: State of Washington, Burlap & Barrel Inc., Basic Fun Inc. |
| May 8, 2026 | Government appeals to CAFC; USTR Greer states confidence in reversal |
| May 12, 2026 | CAFC issues administrative stay suspending CIT's injunction while appeal is considered |
| May 20, 2026 | CIT denies government's motion for a stay pending appeal (government failed to show irreparable harm or likelihood of success) |
| Jun 8, 2026 | CAFC administrative stay remains in force — CBP collecting 10% Section 122 duties from all importers except the 3 named plaintiffs |

**Calculator impact:** None to current rate (`S122-01` remains 10%, endDate `2026-07-23`). The CAFC stay means the rate is effectively still in force. Updated `S122-01.notes` to reflect this status.

**Watch list:** If CAFC lifts the stay or rules against the government before July 24, the Section 122 tariffs could stop being collected — at which point collected duties between Feb 24–[injunction date] would potentially become refundable, significantly expanding calculator scope.
