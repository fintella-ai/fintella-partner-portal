## Finding 1 — CAPE Phase 2 Launch (June 29, 2026) 🔴 HIGH PRIORITY

**Source**: CBP Executive Assistant Commissioner Susan Thomas, testimony at CIT hearing on May 27 order to show cause, June 9, 2026. Confirmed by SmarTrade/Thompson Hines and multiple law firm bulletins.

**What changed**: CBP officially confirmed CAPE Phase 2 launches **June 29, 2026** — two days from now.

**Phase 2 scope**:
- Entry types: 01, 02, 06 (standard consumption, informal, FTZ withdrawal) that are **flagged for reconciliation** (the importer indicated at entry that they would later file a Type 09 reconciliation entry)
- Eligible if: (1) entry is unliquidated OR liquidated within 80 days of Phase 2 filing date, AND (2) the Type 09 reconciliation entry has NOT yet been filed at time of CAPE acceptance
- Estimated coverage: ~2.8 million additional entries, ~$28.7B in refunds
- Combined Phase 1 + Phase 2 total: ~$130B of the $166B in unlawfully collected IEEPA tariffs

**Phase 3** (end of July 2026): Will cover **finally liquidated entries** (>80 days since liquidation). CRITICAL CAVEAT: The government's current position is that Phase 3 refunds will only be issued to importers who **filed CIT lawsuits**. Importers who did not file suit will not receive Phase 3 refunds under the government's contested reading of the court orders. This is being disputed at the Federal Circuit (see Finding 3).

## Finding 3 — Government Appealing IEEPA Refund Order + CIT Section 122 Ruling 🟡 MEDIUM (Monitor)

### 3a. Government Appeals IEEPA Refund Order (June 2, 2026)

**Source**: Holland & Knight "IEEPA Tariff Refund Update" (June 2026); Foley & Lardner (June 2026); Thompson Hines SmarTrade (June 2026).

**What happened**: On June 2, 2026, the DOJ formally appealed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit Senior Judge Richard Eaton's CIT orders directing CBP to refund all $166B in IEEPA tariffs to all importers of record.

**Government's argument**: The CIT's universal refund orders constitute impermissible "universal injunctions" extending relief to importers who did not file protective CIT actions. The government argues it can keep refunds from non-litigant importers.

**CBP's current position**: Phase 3 refunds (finally liquidated entries) will only be paid to importers who filed CIT suits — effectively withholding ~$36B in refunds while the appeal proceeds.

**Impact on calculator**: CAPE Phase 1 and Phase 2 refund processing continues as planned. Phase 3 is the contested category. No immediate change to the calculator's eligibility logic is required beyond what's already noted above.

**Watch**: If the Federal Circuit issues a stay or overturns the CIT refund order, the `litigation` filing path and Phase 3 planning would need revision.

### 3b. CIT Strikes Down Section 122 Tariffs (May 7, 2026)

**Source**: Skadden, Gibson Dunn, Holland & Knight, BDO, Cooley, Barnes Richardson Colburn, ASIL (all dated May 7–21, 2026).

**What happened**: A divided three-judge CIT panel in Oregon v. United States and Burlap and Barrel, Inc. v. United States (May 7, 2026) held the 10%/15% Section 122 tariffs **unlawful** — the court held that the balance-of-payments deficit indicators cited in Proclamation 11012 do not satisfy the statutory threshold in 19 U.S.C. § 2132.

**Scope of relief**: **Extremely limited** — injunction applies only to three named importer plaintiffs (State of Washington, Burlap and Barrel, Inc., Basic Fun, Inc). All other importers are still obligated to pay Section 122 duties.

**Appeal**: Government appealed to the Federal Circuit on May 8, 2026. USTR Greer expressed confidence in prevailing. Briefing schedule not yet public.

**Impact on calculator**: No immediate data change. If the Federal Circuit affirms broadly, Section 122 duties would become refundable for the general import population — the calculator would need a new eligibility path for Section 122 entries (similar to the IEEPA path). Low probability near-term; watch for Federal Circuit ruling.
