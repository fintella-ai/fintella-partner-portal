## Finding 1 — CRITICAL: CIT Invalidates Section 122 Tariffs (May 7, 2026)

**Case:** *Burlap & Barrel, Inc. v. Trump*, consolidated with *Oregon v. Trump*  
**Court:** U.S. Court of International Trade (CIT), three-judge panel  
**Date:** May 7, 2026  
**Decision:** 2-1 ruling that the 10% Section 122 global tariff is unlawful  

### What the CIT held
The CIT held that President Trump's February 2026 Proclamation did not meet the statutory criteria to invoke Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. § 2132). The Court issued a permanent injunction prohibiting CBP from collecting Section 122 duties from the prevailing plaintiffs.

### Scope of the injunction
**Narrow — plaintiff-specific only.** The injunction protects:
- Burlap & Barrel, Inc.
- Basic Fun, Inc.
- State of Washington

All other importers are NOT covered. CBP continues collecting the 10% duty from non-party importers.

### CAFC administrative stay (May 12, 2026)
The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit entered an immediate administrative stay on May 12, suspending the CIT's injunction while the appeal proceeds. Even the named plaintiffs are temporarily subject to continued collection while the CAFC considers a more prolonged stay during the appeal.

### CIT denies government stay motion (May 20, 2026)
The CIT separately denied the government's motion to pause its own order during appeal — this is a distinct procedural posture from the CAFC stay.

### Calculator impact
**No rate change required.** Collections continue for all non-party importers. The `S122-01` entry's `endDate: "2026-07-23"` remains accurate.

**Notes updated.** The `S122-01.notes` field in `ieepa-tariff-rates.json` now documents the CIT ruling, CAFC stay, scope of injunction, and the potential future refund trigger if CAFC upholds the CIT.

**Watch item:** If the CAFC upholds the CIT's ruling and dissolves the stay, Section 122 duties collected from all importers could become broadly refundable — analogous to the IEEPA refund window triggered by the Feb 2026 SCOTUS ruling. At that point, the calculator would need a new `SECTION122-TERMINATION` event entry and potential CAPE Phase expansion to cover Section 122 entries.

## Finding 2 — CAPE Phase 1 Progress (May 2026)

**Status:** Phase 1 active, Phase 2 not yet launched

CBP published an updated ACE Portal CAPE Declarations and Error Definitions document dated May 2026. Key operational details:

- **Phase 1 scope unchanged:** Unliquidated entries + entries within 80 days of liquidation. Entry types 08, 09, 23, 47 excluded — confirmed in the May 2026 update.
- **First-pass acceptance rate:** ~21% as of late May 2026. CBP will not launch Phase 2 until Phase 1 acceptance rates stabilize.
- **Phase 2 scope (expected Q3 2026):** Type 09 reconciliation entries, Type 47 drawback entries, historically liquidated entries outside the 80-day window.
- **60-90 day refund processing window** after CAPE Declaration acceptance remains in effect.

### Calculator impact
**No code change required.** `checkEligibility()` in `tariff-calculator.ts` already correctly excludes types 08, 09, 23, 47 via `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES`. Phase 2 is not yet launched — eligibility code should be updated when CBP formally announces Phase 2 with a CSMS launch message.

## Finding 4 — Section 122 Statutory Expiry Status (July 24, 2026)

Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 limits the President to a maximum 150-day tariff surcharge. The current 10% global surcharge expires **July 24, 2026** unless Congress acts to extend it. As of May 23, 2026:

- No congressional action detected to extend the Section 122 surcharge.
- No White House announcements of intent to invoke an alternative authority if Section 122 expires.
- The CAFC appeal timeline may or may not resolve before July 24.

**Calculator impact:** `S122-01.endDate: "2026-07-23"` remains correct.

**Watch item:** If Congress extends Section 122 or the administration invokes a new authority post-July 24, a new rate entry will be required. Monitor the Federal Register for any proclamation issued in late June or early July 2026.
