## Finding 1: CIT Euro-Notions Orders — May 27, 2026 (YESTERDAY)

**Significance: HIGH — CAPE Phase 2 Trigger Watch**

On May 27, 2026, Judge Richard Eaton (the sole judge designated to hear all IEEPA refund cases) issued two orders in *Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. United States* (CIT No. 25-00595):

- **CBP must file a short report by June 10, 2026** explaining its position on issuing IEEPA refunds for ALL entries, including those with "finally liquidated" status (i.e., liquidated more than 80 days before CAPE Phase 1 acceptance, which places them outside Phase 1's protest-window eligibility).
- **Closed settlement conference scheduled June 11, 2026** immediately following CBP's report.

The Court expressed particular concern about millions of **informal entries** (entry type "11") where liquidation occurred simultaneously or near-simultaneously with the entry date, making those entries finally liquidated well before CAPE Phase 1 launched on April 20, 2026.

**What this means for the calculator:**
- No eligibility rule changes today. The `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` set and the 80-day liquidation protest window in `checkEligibility()` remain correct for CAPE Phase 1.
- **Watch for CBP's June 10 response.** If CBP proposes expanding CAPE to cover finally-liquidated entries (a Phase 2 mechanism), the `checkEligibility()` function will need updating to remove or soften the `excluded_expired` path, and `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` may need to change.
- The `excluded_expired` status currently sends users to `legal_required` routing. If a Phase 2 administrative process is announced, a new `eligible_phase2` status path may be needed.

**Next monitor date:** June 10–12, 2026 (after CBP's court filing and settlement conference).

## Finding 2: Section 122 CIT Invalidation + CAFC Stay — May 7–20, 2026

**Significance: HIGH — Data Notes Updated; No Rate Change**

A series of rulings on the Section 122 replacement tariff:

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| May 7, 2026 | CIT issued 2-1 opinion holding Section 122 tariffs unlawful (*Burlap & Barrel Inc., Basic Fun Inc., State of Washington v. United States*). Permanent injunction issued — but limited to the three prevailing plaintiffs only. |
| May 8, 2026 | Government immediately appealed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC). |
| May 12, 2026 | CAFC granted a **temporary administrative stay** of the CIT injunction. Section 122 tariffs continue to be collected from all importers pending full appellate review. |
| May 20, 2026 | CIT denied the government's separate motion to stay its own ruling. CAFC stay remains controlling. |

**Practical impact today:** Section 122 (9903.03.01, 10% global surcharge) is still in full force and effect for all importers. The CIT ruling applies only to the three named plaintiffs, and even they are currently paying under the CAFC administrative stay. The July 24, 2026 expiry date is unchanged.

**Impact on calculator:** No rate change. No eligibility change. The S122-01 seed record notes have been updated in this commit to reflect the legal status accurately. Section 122 duties remain outside CAPE scope (CAPE only covers IEEPA duties).

**Watch for:** CAFC ruling on the merits of the Section 122 appeal. If CAFC affirms the CIT, Section 122 refunds could become a new legal pathway for importers — potentially a new calculator feature.

## Finding 3: CBP FAQ Reconciliation Update — May 26, 2026

**Significance: LOW — Confirms existing code logic; no change needed**

CBP updated its IEEPA FAQ page on May 26, 2026 with two new Q&A entries addressing reconciliation entries. Key clarifications:

1. Entries flagged for reconciliation (types 01, 02, 06) where **no Type 09 has yet been filed** can participate in CAPE Phase 1. CBP recommends the trade community hold Type 09 filings unless the reconciliation deadline is within 30 days, so the CAPE declaration can be processed first.
2. If the reconciliation deadline is imminent (<30 days), trade should file the Type 09 and deposit duties excluding IEEPA — the refund process will follow via Phase 2.

**Impact on calculator:** None. The `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` set correctly excludes entry type `"09"` (the reconciliation summary entry itself). The CBP clarification is about the *underlying* consumption entries (01, 02, 06) that are pending reconciliation — those are already eligible in Phase 1. No code change needed.
