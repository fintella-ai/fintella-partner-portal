## Finding 2 — Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs: New Regime Since July 24, 2026 ✅ Reference Entry Added

**Status:** Active — confirmed  
**Source:** [Section 301 Is Dead, Section 301 Took Its Place Overnight | FreightFigures](https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-live-july-24-2026)  
**Source 2:** [CSMS #69326983 — Section 301 Forced Labor Import Duties](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/421d887)  
**Source 3:** [Section 301 Forced Labor Tariffs on 60 Economies | GHY International](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/ustr-section-301-forced-labor-60-economies/)

At the same minute Section 122 expired (12:01 AM ET July 24, 2026), USTR-issued Section 301 forced-labor tariffs took effect — no tariff-free gap:

- **Authority:** Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. § 2411)
- **HTS codes:** 9903.05.20 through 9903.05.92
- **Rates:**
  - **10%** — 17 economies with partial forced-labor import protections
  - **12.5%** — 38 economies without forced-labor protections
- **Coverage:** ~60 economies, ~99.4% of U.S. imports
- **Exempt categories:** aluminum, steel, copper, passenger vehicles, light trucks, medium/heavy-duty vehicles, wood products, semiconductor articles
- **Specific country notes:** UK at 10%; Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, EU27 capped at 10% per bilateral arrangements

**⚠️ Critical distinction:** These tariffs are **NOT IEEPA tariffs** and are **NOT refundable via CAPE**. They should never appear in refund calculations.

**What was updated:**
- `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json`: Added `S301-FORCED-LABOR-01` as a `rateType: "section301_forced_labor"` reference entry with a prominent "NON-IEEPA, NON-REFUNDABLE" note.
- Updated `_metadata.version` to `1.1.0` and `generatedDate` to `2026-08-22`.
- Added `_metadata.sources` entries for the CAPE Phase 2 and CSMS #69127837 changes.

**John to consider:** The TIE calculator UI may want to display a banner to importers explaining the current Section 301 regime when they ask about tariffs paid after July 24, 2026, directing them to note these are NOT refundable. Whether to add that UI notice is out of scope for this automated run.

## Finding 3 — CAPE Warehouse Entry Exclusion (Types 21 & 22), July 7, 2026 ✅ Fixed

**Status:** In effect since July 7, 2026 — confirmed  
**Source:** [CBP CSMS #69127837 — Warehouse Entry CAPE Guidance](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/automated/cargo-systems-messaging-service) (via CBP CSMS archive)  
**Source 2:** [CBP Launches CAPE for IEEPA Duty Refunds | GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/cbp-cape-ieepa-refund-progress/)

Effective July 7, 2026, CBP ceased accepting warehouse entries (Types 21 and 22) on CAPE Declarations. Per CSMS #69127837, these entry types were excluded from the CAPE refund process.

**What was wrong:** `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts` was `["08", "09", "23", "47"]`. Types 21 and 22 were missing, meaning warehouse entries were being incorrectly flagged as `eligible` via CAPE Phase 1.

**What was updated:**
- `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`: Added `"21"` and `"22"` to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES`. Updated the comment to document Phase 2 reconciliation-flagged entry handling and the CAPE Phase 2 TODO for `EntryForEligibility`.

**Functional impact:** Any importer who submitted a warehouse entry number through the TIE calculator after July 7, 2026, would have received an incorrect "eligible" verdict. After this fix, Type 21/22 entries correctly return `excluded_type`.

## Finding 4 — CAPE Phase 3 Postponed (Announced August 21, 2026) ℹ️ Watch Item

**Status:** Postponed — no new launch date  
**Source:** [Breaking Trade News: CAPE Phase 3 Postponed, Canada 338 Tariffs Delayed | Customs & International Trade Law Blog](https://customsandinternationaltradelaw.com/2026/08/21/breaking-trade-news-cape-phase-3-postponed-canada-338-tariffs-delayed/)  
**Source 2:** [CAPE Phase 3 for IEEPA Refunds On Track for End of July | Green Worldwide Shipping](https://www.greenworldwide.com/cape-phase-3-for-ieepa-tariff-refunds-on-track-for-end-of-july-as-federal-circuit-appeal-continues/)

CAPE Phase 3 (covering **finally-liquidated entries** for importers who filed CIT lawsuits) was originally scheduled for August 20, 2026 and was postponed indefinitely as of August 21, 2026 — yesterday.

**Background:**
- CIT July 15-17, 2026 orders: Judge Eaton ordered CBP to reliquidate finally-liquidated entries (liquidated > 80 days) for all ~3,700 pending IEEPA plaintiffs.
- Phase 3 is available **only** to importers who filed individual lawsuits at the CIT — NOT a universal refund channel.
- The government's Federal Circuit appeal (Euro-Notions Florida, Case No. 26-1898, filed June 2, 2026) remains pending and does not stay Phase 3, but adds uncertainty about the universal refund order.
- As of August 4, 2026: ~$128.68B had entered CAPE for processing; ~$100B certified and sent to Treasury for disbursement (~60% of total pool paid).

**What was NOT changed:** The calculator's `checkEligibility()` function correctly maps these entries:
- Entries with `liquidationDate` where protest window (180 days) has NOT expired → `filingMethod: "protest"` or `"cape_phase1"` depending on the 80-day CAPE window
- Entries where protest window HAS expired → `filingMethod: "litigation"` (CIT-only)

The Phase 3 postponement does not change the eligibility mapping — it only affects the *timing* of when CBP will process the refund for the `litigation` cohort. No code change is needed today.

**Recommendation for John:** Consider whether the calculator should surface a "Phase 3 delayed — estimated timing TBD" notice on the `litigation` path detail screen.

## Finding 5 — CIT / Federal Circuit: Euro-Notions Appeal Pending ℹ️ Watch Item

**Source:** [IEEPA Tariff Refund Update: Government Appeals CIT Refund Order | Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/ieepa-tariff-refund-update-government-appeals)

The government's appeal of the CIT's universal refund injunction (Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. United States, Fed. Cir. Case No. 26-1898, filed June 2, 2026) is pending at the Federal Circuit. No ruling has issued as of August 22, 2026.

The central legal question: whether the CIT's order requiring refunds to ALL importers (not just those who sued) is an impermissible universal injunction. Resolution would affect whether `litigation`-path importers who have not filed suit have a viable refund path at all.

**No code change needed.** Monitor for a Federal Circuit ruling — if the court restricts refunds to only CIT plaintiffs, the calculator's `litigation` path guidance would need updating to add a notice about the need to file at CIT before the Federal Circuit rules.
