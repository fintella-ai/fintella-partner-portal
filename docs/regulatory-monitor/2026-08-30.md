## Finding 1 — ACTIONABLE: Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs Replace Section 122 (Effective July 24, 2026)

### What happened

Section 122 expired at 12:01 AM ET July 24, 2026 after its statutory 150-day maximum (as expected and already reflected in the seed data with `endDate: "2026-07-23"`).

**At the exact same moment**, new Section 301 forced-labor tariffs took effect under a two-tier structure:

| Tier | Rate | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 10% | ~30 economies that impose a forced-labor import ban but don't fully enforce it, or that have made commitments in bilateral trade agreements |
| Tier 2 | 12.5% | ~30 economies that have made no meaningful effort to police forced labor |

**~60 economies total** are covered. Tier 1 includes: EU member states, Canada, Mexico, Indonesia, India (partial), UK, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Jordan, Malaysia, Taiwan, and others. Tier 2 covers countries with no forced-labor enforcement regime.

### Legal authority

- **Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974** (19 U.S.C. § 2411) — USTR found each country's failure to prohibit forced-labor goods constitutes an "unreasonable act, policy, or practice" burdening US commerce
- **Federal Register:** 2026-15181, published July 28, 2026
- **USTR Press Release:** https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations
- **USTR Fact Sheet:** https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/july/fact-sheet-ustr-section-301-action-response-failure-60-economies-ban-imports-produced-forced-labor
- **CBP CSMS #69326983:** Section 301 Forced Labor Import Duties guidance (see https://cbp.gov/trade/automated/cargo-systems-messaging-service)

### HTS chapter 99 codes

- **9903.05.20 through 9903.05.84** — country-specific codes assigned per economy
- **Exemptions:** goods already subject to Section 232 tariffs; certain products in Annex A (pharmaceuticals, some electronics)
- Patented pharmaceuticals also added to exemptions effective July 31, 2026

### Impact on TIE calculator

**These are Section 301 tariffs, not IEEPA tariffs.** They are NOT covered by CAPE and are NOT refundable under the IEEPA refund program. Therefore:
- `checkEligibility()` in `tariff-calculator.ts` — **no changes needed** (IEEPA date window Feb 1, 2025–Feb 23, 2026 is unchanged; Section 301 tariffs fall outside it)
- `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` — **no changes needed**
- `ieepa-tariff-rates.json` — **updated in this PR**: `S122-01.supersededBy` changed from `null` to `"S301-FL-TIER1"`, and two new Section 301 entries (`S301-FL-TIER1`, `S301-FL-TIER2`) added to document the current tariff regime

### What needs John's review

- The new seed entries provide the "what replaced Section 122" context. If the calculator's UI has a "current tariff rate" display, it now shows these Section 301 rates as the applicable surcharge for new imports.
- The country-to-code mapping (which exact 9903.05.XX code applies to which country) is in Annex A of FR 2026-15181 — this PR does NOT enumerate all 60 country-specific codes (that would require parsing a multi-hundred-page Federal Register notice). The two tier-level entries added are sufficient for eligibility context; country-specific codes can be added in a follow-up if the calculator needs rate lookup for current (Section 301) imports.

## Finding 2 — NOTEWORTHY (no code change): CIT Ruled Section 122 Invalid — Appeal Pending

### What happened

On **May 7, 2026**, the U.S. Court of International Trade ruled **2-1** that the Section 122 surcharge exceeded presidential authority — the current economic conditions don't meet the statutory threshold of "large and serious balance-of-payments deficits." Source: Skadden analysis at https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs

**However:**
- The injunction is **limited to 3 named plaintiffs** only (State of Washington, Burlap and Barrel Inc., Basic Fun Inc.) — not class-wide
- The CAFC issued an administrative **stay on May 12, 2026** while it considers the government's appeal — the government continued collecting Section 122 duties through the July 24 expiry
- Section 122 has **already expired**, limiting practical impact going forward

### Impact on TIE calculator

**No code changes needed at this time.** If the CAFC upholds the CIT ruling (overturning the stay), this could create refund opportunities for non-plaintiff importers for duties paid Feb 24 – Jul 23, 2026 under Section 122. That would require:
1. A new CAPE-like mechanism for Section 122 refunds (no such system exists yet)
2. Possibly a new `rateType: "section122_refund"` eligibility check

**Monitor this:** If the CAFC rules, a follow-up PR will be needed to add Section 122 refund eligibility logic. Ward & Smith article: https://www.wardandsmith.com/article/court-of-international-trade-rejects-10-section-122-tariff-what-businesses-should-know-while-the-appeal-proceeds
