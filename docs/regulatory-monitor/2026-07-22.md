## Finding 1 — CAPE Phase 3 launches end of July 2026 for finally liquidated entries (ACTIONABLE)

**Source:** CBP testimony at CIT June 9, 2026 hearing; CIT order July 17, 2026 (Euro-Notions Florida v. United States, No. 25-595)

**Summary:**
- CBP confirmed at the June 9, 2026 CIT hearing that **CAPE Phase 3** will be ready **by end of July 2026**.
- Phase 3 covers **finally liquidated entries** — entries liquidated more than 180 days ago on which IEEPA duties were paid. This represents approximately **$11.4 billion** in IEEPA duties.
- On **July 17, 2026**, CIT Senior Judge Richard Eaton issued an order directing CBP to reliquidate entries that have been liquidated >80 days for plaintiffs who have filed CIT cases.
- **Key restriction:** The government (DOJ) continues to argue CBP lacks authority to reliquidate finally liquidated entries without an individual CIT lawsuit. Per the July 17 order, CAPE Phase 3 processing currently applies only to companies that have **filed a CIT case**.
- The government's appeal of the broader universal refund order is pending at the **Federal Circuit**.

**Prior state:** The `checkEligibility()` function in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts` categorized entries liquidated >180 days as `status: "excluded_expired"` with `filingMethod: "litigation"` — implying only multi-year court fights with no near-term resolution.

**Code change made:**
- Added `"cape_phase3"` to the `FilingMethod` union type with full JSDoc explaining the CIT case requirement and Federal Circuit appeal.
- Updated the `checkEligibility()` logic for entries with `daysRemaining < 0` (past the 180-day protest window): now returns `filingMethod: "cape_phase3"` with `needsReview: true` and a `reviewNote` explaining the CIT filing requirement and government appeal.
- The `status` remains `"excluded_expired"` (so the routing bucket continues to show `"legal_required"` — these entries genuinely need a lawyer) but the filing path is now `"cape_phase3"` rather than generic `"litigation"`.

**File changed:** `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`

## Finding 2 — Section 122 tariff expires July 24, 2026 (2 days from today)

**Source:** Section 122 of Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. § 2132); multiple trade law firm analyses confirmed July 24 expiration.

**Summary:**
- The Section 122 global surcharge (10%, HTS 9903.03.01) was imposed February 24, 2026 to replace IEEPA tariffs after the SCOTUS ruling.
- Section 122 has a **statutory 150-day maximum duration**. Counting from February 24: expires at **12:01 AM EDT on July 24, 2026** — two days from today.
- **No Congressional extension** has been enacted. No extension legislation is pending.
- **Replacement plan:** USTR had a July 20 deadline to complete Section 301 investigations targeting 46 countries at 12.5%. If the Section 301 action is not finalized in time, rates temporarily revert to MFN plus existing Section 232/301 surcharges until the replacement lands.
- **Does NOT affect IEEPA refund calculator logic.** Section 122 duties (collected Feb 24–July 24, 2026) are a different instrument from IEEPA duties (Feb 2025–Feb 2026). CAPE only processes IEEPA duties.

**Prior state:** The seed data entry `S122-01` already had the correct `endDate: "2026-07-23"` (last day in effect). Notes were sparse on what happens after expiration.

**Seed data change made:**
- Updated `S122-01.notes` to document the confirmed July 24 expiration, no-extension status, the Section 301 replacement plan, and an explicit note that Section 122 duties are NOT refundable via CAPE.
- Updated `supersededBy` from `null` to `"S301-REPLACEMENT-TBD"` to signal the replacement context in the data model.

**File changed:** `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json`
