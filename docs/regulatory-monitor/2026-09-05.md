## 1. Section 122 Tariff Expired (July 24, 2026) — DATA UPDATE

**Status**: Expired as anticipated. Database updated.

**What happened**:
- The 10% Section 122 global surcharge (HTS 9903.03.01) hit its 150-day statutory cap and expired at 12:01 AM ET on July 24, 2026.
- No replacement Section 122 proclamation was issued.
- Side note: The U.S. Court of International Trade ruled 2-1 on May 7, 2026 that the Section 122 tariffs were unlawful (balance-of-payments conditions not met); that ruling was stayed pending appeal and became moot on expiration.

**What replaced it**:
- USTR finalized a Section 301 forced-labor action covering ~60 economies, effective **simultaneously** with Section 122's expiration (July 24, 2026, 12:01 AM ET) — no gap.
- Two-tier rate structure:
  - **10%** additional ad valorem — 17 economies (HTS 9903.05.20)
  - **12.5%** additional ad valorem — 38 economies (HTS 9903.05.21)
- Special combined-rate treatment (not simple stack): EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland, Taiwan
- Exemptions: USMCA/CAFTA-DR goods, civil aircraft, pharmaceuticals, certain electronics (9903.05.85–9903.05.99, 9903.06.01–9903.06.21)
- Authority: Trade Act of 1974 §301 (19 U.S.C. 2411), NOT IEEPA
- CBP CSMS: #69326983

**⚠️ Important for calculator**:
- Section 301 tariffs are **NOT IEEPA** — entries made only under Section 301 are NOT eligible for IEEPA refunds via CAPE
- The new entries added to the seed file are for display/context only; the calculator should not offer refund paths for them

**Seed data changes**:
- `S122-01.supersededBy` updated from `null` → `"S301-FL-01"`
- `S122-01.notes` updated to reflect expiration and CIT ruling
- Two new entries added: `S301-FL-01` (10% baseline) and `S301-FL-02` (12.5% elevated)
- Metadata version bumped 1.0.0 → 1.1.0

## 2. CAPE Phase 2 — Reconciliation Entries (Live June 29, 2026) — CODE UPDATE

**Status**: Live since June 29, 2026. Calculator eligibility logic updated.

**What changed**:
- Effective June 29, 2026, CBP CAPE accepts entry types **01, 02, 06** that were **flagged for reconciliation** where the corresponding **Type-09 reconciliation entry has not yet been filed**.
- Phase 1 (launched April 20, 2026) already covered unliquidated entries and those liquidated within 80 days.
- The same 80-day liquidation window applies to Phase 2 entries.
- Type 09 entries themselves remain excluded from CAPE (they are the reconciliation entry forms, not refund-eligible entries).

**Code changes** (`src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`):
- `FilingMethod` union type: added `"cape_phase2"` variant
- `EntryForEligibility` interface: added `isFlaggedForReconciliation?: boolean` field
- Added constants: `CAPE_PHASE2_RECONCILIATION_ENTRY_TYPES`, `CAPE_PHASE2_LAUNCH_DATE`
- `checkEligibility()`: new step 4 routes reconciliation-flagged 01/02/06 entries to `cape_phase2` before the general exclusion check; updated comments throughout

## 3. CAPE Phase 3 — Temporarily Delayed (Announced Aug 25, 2026) — NO CODE CHANGE

**Status**: Delayed. No code change needed (litigation path remains correct).

**What happened**:
- CBP announced on August 25, 2026 that CAPE Phase 3 (covering **finally-liquidated entries**, primarily for importers who filed CIT lawsuits) is temporarily delayed.
- Reason: CBP is building additional validations to prevent inadvertent changes to non-IEEPA duties during reliquidation, and to handle cases where IEEPA duties were reported incorrectly on original entries.
- No new deployment date announced as of this report.

**CAPE stats as of Aug 21, 2026**:
- 272,029 CAPE declarations submitted; 191,494 passed file validations
- 26.4 million entries accepted for IEEPA duty removal
- $132.5 billion in refunds accepted for processing; $106.6 billion sent to Treasury
- 22,170 refunds (~$1.7 billion) on hold due to missing ACH bank account information

**Calculator impact**: None. Entries past the 180-day protest window still correctly route to `filingMethod: "litigation"`. CAPE Phase 3 would have been a separate path for CIT litigants only.

## 4. Federal Circuit Ruling — All Importers Entitled to IEEPA Refunds (August 2026) — NO CODE CHANGE

**Status**: Ruling affirmed. No code change needed.

**What happened**:
- The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit affirmed the CIT's orders in August 2026.
- All importers who paid IEEPA tariffs (fentanyl-related EOs and "Liberation Day" reciprocal EOs) are entitled to refunds **without having to file separate lawsuits**.
- The government had argued the CIT's refund orders were impermissible "universal injunctions" — the Federal Circuit rejected this.
- CAPE remains the primary refund mechanism; the ruling strengthens the legal basis that CBP is obligated to process all eligible entries.

**Calculator impact**: None. This confirms existing behavior — all eligible entries in the IEEPA period should receive refunds. No eligibility narrowing.

**Sources**:
