## Findings

### 1. CAPE: Warehouse Entries (Types 21/22) Excluded — EFFECTIVE JULY 7, 2026 [CODE CHANGE]

**Source**: CSMS #69127837 — UPDATE: CAPE for IEEPA Refunds – Warehouse Entries  
**Date**: Effective July 7, 2026

Effective July 7, 2026, warehouse entries (Entry Types 21 and 22) are no longer accepted on CAPE Declarations and will be rejected with "ENTRY TYPE NOT ALLOWED." This is because IEEPA duties on warehoused goods are collected at the time of withdrawal, not at time of warehouse entry. The correct path is to submit warehouse withdrawal entries (Types 31, 32, 34, 38) on the CAPE Declaration instead.

**Important transition note**: Warehouse entries (Types 21/22) that were accepted on CAPE between April 20 – July 6, 2026 without a corresponding withdrawal submission will NOT receive IEEPA refunds. Those filers must submit separate CAPE Declarations covering the warehouse withdrawal entries.

**Calculator change**: Added "21" and "22" to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`. The excluded_type reason for these entry types now directs users to submit withdrawal entries (31/32/34/38) instead.

### 2. CAPE Phase 2 — Launched June 29, 2026 [INFORMATIONAL / COMMENT UPDATE]

**Source**: CBP announcements; SmarTrade (thompsonhinesmartrade.com, Jun 2026)  
**Date**: Deployed June 29, 2026

CAPE Phase 2 expanded the system to accept entries flagged for reconciliation (Entry Types 01, 02, 06) where the reconciliation entry (Type 09) has NOT yet been filed. The CAPE process removes IEEPA duties from these flagged entries prior to the filing of the reconciliation entry. Eligibility criteria remain the same: unliquidated entries or entries within 80 days of liquidation.

**What is NOT included in Phase 2**: Reconciliation-flagged entries where a Type 09 has already been filed — those are deferred to a future phase.

**Calculator impact**: The current eligibility logic already handles Types 01, 02, 06 correctly (they are not in `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES`). Updated JSDoc comments to acknowledge Phase 2 explicitly.

### 3. CAPE Phase 3 — Expected Late July 2026, but Legally Uncertain [INFORMATIONAL]

**Source**: CIT hearing June 9, 2026; internationaltradetoday.com; greenworldwide.com  
**Date**: Expected late July 2026

Phase 3 will technically cover finally liquidated entries (entries liquidated more than 80 days ago). Programming is expected to be ready by end of July 2026.

**CRITICAL CAVEAT**: CBP reversed its earlier position on May 29, 2026, asserting it lacks legal authority to issue IEEPA refunds on finally liquidated entries without an importer-specific court judgment. The DOJ has appealed the CIT's broad reliquidation order to the Federal Circuit. CBP confirmed at the June 9 CIT hearing that Phase 3 refunds will only be processed for importers who have filed individual CIT suits.

**Calculator impact**: The current `filingMethod: "litigation"` for entries liquidated >180 days ago remains CORRECT — litigation is still the required path. Updated JSDoc comment to note the Phase 3 situation. No code change needed.

### 4. Section 122 Tariff Expiry — July 24, 2026 (13 days away) [DATA NOTE UPDATE]

**Source**: Multiple trade law sources; Federal Register 2026-03824; statutory text  
**Date**: Expires July 24, 2026

The 10% Section 122 global surcharge expires July 24, 2026 by operation of law — the 150-day statutory maximum under 19 U.S.C. § 2132. The President CANNOT extend it unilaterally. Congressional extension is considered unlikely (bipartisan appetite is low; "Reclaim Trade Powers Act" moves in opposite direction).

The calculator's seed data (`S122-01`) already has the correct `endDate: "2026-07-23"`. Updated notes to clarify the hard expiry, the unextendability, and the proposed Section 301 replacement (see Finding 5).

### 5. Section 301 Proposed Replacement — Not Yet Finalized [INFORMATIONAL / DATA NOTE]

**Source**: Federal Register 2026-11296 (June 5, 2026); USTR press release June 2, 2026;  
tariffstool.com; dorsey.com  
**Date**: USTR determination deadline July 20, 2026

USTR launched two Section 301 investigations in March 2026, covering 60 economies on forced-labor import-ban compliance and excess manufacturing capacity. After public comment (450+ submissions) and hearings (July 7, 2026), USTR must issue a determination by July 20, 2026.

Proposed rates:
- **12.5%** on 46 countries lacking effective forced-labor import bans (includes Algeria, Angola, Brazil, China, India, Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, and others)
- **10%** on countries with partial ART (Agreement on Reciprocal Trade) commitments

Key legal difference from Section 122: **No rate cap, no time limit** under Section 301.

**Status as of July 11, 2026**: PROPOSAL ONLY. Hearings completed July 7. USTR determination due July 20. Final notice must be published in Federal Register before rates take effect. A Section 301 replacement tariff would be a separate, new regime — not related to IEEPA refunds.

**Calculator impact**: No code change yet — Section 301 is not finalized. Watch the Federal Register for a USTR final determination week of July 20. If finalized, a new rate entry should be added (but note: Section 301 rates are NOT IEEPA and not refundable under the CAPE process).
