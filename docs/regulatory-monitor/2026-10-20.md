## 1. CAPE Phase 3 — Launched October 6, 2026 ✅ CODE UPDATED

**Source:** Thompson Hine, "CBP Launches Phase 2 of the IEEPA Tariff Refund Process and Confirms Development of Phase 3" (https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/cbp-launches-phase-2-of-the-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-and-confirms-development-of-phase-3/); GHY Trade Compliance, Oct 5, 2026 update (https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/cbp-cape-ieepa-refund-progress/)

**What changed:** CBP deployed CAPE Phase 3 on October 6, 2026. Phase 3 currently applies only to importers who are plaintiffs in pending CIT litigation **and** for whom CBP has issued a reliquidation order. Entries previously classified as "litigation only" (protest window expired, >180 days since liquidation) may now have a CAPE Phase 3 path if the importer meets these criteria.

**Code change:** `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`
- Added `"cape_phase3"` to the `FilingMethod` type with documentation
- Updated the `excluded_expired` / `filingMethod: "litigation"` branch to set `needsReview: true` and include a `reviewNote` informing clients to check CAPE Phase 3 eligibility
- Updated `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` comment to document Phase 2 and Phase 3 scope

**No change to `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` values** — Phase 3 doesn't open new entry types; it opens a new channel for entries that were already in the system but beyond the protest window.

## 2. CIT Class Action Certified — October 8, 2026 ✅ CODE UPDATED (via #1 above)

**Source:** Miller Chevalier, "Trade Compliance Flash: Court of International Trade Certifies Class Action for IEEPA Tariff Refunds" (https://www.millerchevalier.com/publication/trade-compliance-flash-court-international-trade-certifies-class-action-ieepa-tariff); Steptoe, "Relief in Sight: CIT Certifies Class for IEEPA Tariff Refunds" (https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/global-trade-and-investment-law-blog/relief-in-sight-cit-certifies-class-for-ieepa-tariff-refunds-for-certain-importers.html); CIT Slip Op. 26-123 (https://www.cit.uscourts.gov/sites/cit/files/26-123.pdf)

**What changed:** On October 8, 2026, CIT Judge Eaton certified the lead refund case (Freestyle World v. United States, Slip Op. 26-123) as a class action covering importers who paid IEEPA tariffs. Class membership may qualify importers for CAPE Phase 3 reliquidation orders. The certification is subject to Federal Circuit appeal and does not itself guarantee refunds — class members receive refunds only if the court enters a class-wide injunction and CBP processes entries.

**Code change:** The `reviewNote` added in change #1 above references the class certification directly, directing clients to check whether they qualify as class members for CAPE Phase 3 access.

## 3. Section 122 Tariffs — Expired July 24, 2026 (as expected)

**Source:** Strix Smart, "Section 122 Refunds: Where the Appeal Stands as of September 8, 2026" (https://strixsmart.com/resources/blog/section-122-refund-status); PwC, "US Court of International Trade strikes down section 122 tariffs" (https://www.pwc.com/ca/en/services/tax/publications/tax-insights/us-court-strike-down-section-122-tariffs-2026.html)

**Status:** Section 122 tariffs expired July 24, 2026, per the 150-day statutory cap. The seed data already reflects this (`S122-01` has `endDate: "2026-07-23"`). In *Oregon v. United States* (Slip Op. 26-47), CIT held Section 122 tariffs exceeded presidential authority, but the Federal Circuit stayed the injunction on June 11, 2026 pending appeal. As of early October 2026, no Section 122 refunds have been ordered for non-plaintiffs. **No code change needed.**

## 6. IRS Quarterly Interest Rates — Q3 and Q4 2026 Missing from Seed Data ✅ DATA UPDATED

**Source:** IRS Newsroom, "Interest rates remain the same for the fourth quarter of 2026" (https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/interest-rates-remain-the-same-for-the-fourth-quarter-of-2026); IRS, "Quarterly interest rates" (https://www.irs.gov/payments/quarterly-interest-rates); Federal Register, "Quarterly IRS Interest Rates Used in Calculating Interest on Overdue Accounts and Refunds of Customs Duties" (https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/01/2026-13298/quarterly-irs-interest-rates-used-in-calculating-interest-on-overdue-accounts-and-refunds-of-customs)

**What changed:** The `irsInterestRates.quarters` array in `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json` stopped at Q2 2026. Refund interest is compounded daily through the date CBP issues the refund — which for Phase 1 entries filed in mid-2026 could extend into Q3/Q4 2026. Missing quarters cause the calculator to understate interest for those entries.

Confirmed rates from IRS Revenue Rulings 2026-10 and 2026-XX:

| Quarter | Overpayment (individual) | Overpayment (corporate) | Overpayment (corp >$10k) | Underpayment | Fed short-term |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 2026 (Jul 1 – Sep 30) | 7% | 6% | 4.5% | 7% | 4% |
| Q4 2026 (Oct 1 – Dec 31) | 7% | 6% | 4.5% | 7% | 4% |

Note: Q2 2026 was lower (6% overpayment / 6% underpayment) due to a lower federal short-term rate that quarter. Both Q3 and Q4 returned to the pre-Q2 rate of 7%.

**Data change:** Added Q3 2026 and Q4 2026 entries to `irsInterestRates.quarters` in `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json`.
