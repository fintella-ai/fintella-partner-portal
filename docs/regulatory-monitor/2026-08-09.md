## 2. Section 301 Forced Labor Tariffs Replace Section 122 — NEW ✅ (Data updated)

**Source:** USTR Final Action published Federal Register July 23, 2026. CBP CSMS #69326983.  
Multiple confirmed: [Honigman LLP](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462), [Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/forced-labor-meet-section-301-new-tariffs-target-60-of-americas-biggest-trading-partners/), [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/forced-labour-section-301-final-action), [Akerman LLP](https://www.akerman.com/en/perspectives/section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-set-to-cover-most-imports-into-the-u-s-starting.html)

**What happened:** USTR completed 60 Section 301 investigations (forced labor enforcement failures) and imposed additional duties effective July 24, 2026 at 12:01 AM EDT — precisely as Section 122 expired, with no gap.

**Rate structure:**
- **Tier A (10% additional):** Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad & Tobago, United Kingdom, and others
- **Tier B (12.5% additional):** China, Vietnam, and ~40 other economies
- **Threshold treatment** (duty sufficient to bring combined rate to 10%, not added on top): EU, Japan, Korea, Switzerland, Taiwan
- **HTS codes:** 9903.05.20 – 9903.05.84 (country-specific within range)
- **USMCA-origin goods:** Exempt
- **Product exclusions:** Broad — civil aircraft, pharmaceuticals, autos, wood, semiconductors, 471+ specific HTS subheadings added after public comment (U.S. Note 52, Chapter 99)
- **In-transit exemption:** Goods loaded before July 24, entered before July 28, are exempt

**⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE — Not refundable via CAPE:** Section 301 forced labor tariffs are an ongoing Section 301 duty program, NOT IEEPA tariffs. Importers cannot claim CAPE refunds on these duties. The tariff calculator covers IEEPA entry dates (Feb 1, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026) only; no entry in the IEEPA refund window is subject to Section 301 forced labor tariffs.

**Legal challenge:** Liberty Justice Center filed a challenge to Section 301 forced labor tariffs in July 2026 ([source](https://libertyjusticecenter.org/pressrelease/liberty-justice-center-challenges-unlawful-section-301-tariffs-as-administration-replaces-expiring-section-122-tariffs/)), but no stay has been issued. Tariffs are in effect.

**Database change:** Added `S301-FL-TIER-A` and `S301-FL-TIER-B` entries to `ieepa-tariff-rates.json` as informational/successor-regime reference records. Added Section 301 legal authority and sources to `_metadata`.

**John's action needed:** Review whether the calculator's UI should surface a note to importers that entries after July 23, 2026 face Section 301 (not refundable) rather than Section 122. The rate lookup already handles this correctly via date filtering, but a UI callout might be useful.

## 3. CAPE Phase 2 Launched June 29, 2026 — NEW ✅ (Eligibility updated)

**Source:** CBP announcement June 9, 2026. CSMS message. Confirmed:  
[Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/06/cbp-confirms-june-29-2026-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-phase-2-launch/), [PPAI](https://www.ppai.org/media-hub/phase-2-of-tariff-refunds-to-start-june-29/), [customs law blog](https://customsandinternationaltradelaw.com/2026/06/26/cape-reconciliation-refunds/)

**What happened:** Effective June 29, 2026, CAPE began accepting entries flagged for reconciliation (entry types 01, 02, 06) for which the Type 09 reconciliation entry has NOT yet been filed. This is a significant expansion — these entries were previously unable to be processed through CAPE even though they are standard consumption entries.

**Key rule — filing order is critical:** Once the Type 09 reconciliation entry is filed, the underlying flagged entries are permanently locked out of CAPE. CAPE submission must happen BEFORE the Type 09 filing.

**Scope:** Same eligibility window as Phase 1 — unliquidated entries or entries within 80 days of liquidation. Estimated $28.7 billion in additional refunds.

**Note:** Type 09 entries themselves remain excluded from CAPE. Only the underlying consumption entries (01/02/06) that are flagged for reconciliation are eligible via Phase 2.

**Eligibility change:** Updated `checkEligibility()` in `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`:
- Added `isFlaggedForReconciliation?: boolean` field to `EntryForEligibility`
- Added Phase 2 handling block (step 4b) — returns `filingMethod: "cape_phase1"` with `needsReview: true` and a filing-order warning in `reviewNote`
- Updated the excluded Type 09 reason text to clarify the distinction

## 4. CAPE Phase 3 — Court-Ordered Reliquidation for Finally-Liquidated Entries ✅ (Eligibility updated)

**Source:** CIT Senior Judge Eaton order July 17, 2026. CBP progress report due August 4, 2026.  
Confirmed: [Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/07/cit-orders-cbp-to-process-ieepa-tariff-refunds-for-phase-3-finally-liquidated-entries/), [Green Worldwide](https://www.greenworldwide.com/cape-phase-3-for-ieepa-tariff-refunds-on-track-for-end-of-july-as-federal-circuit-appeal-continues/), [Diaz Trade Law](https://diaztradelaw.com/ieepa-cape-phase-three-refunds/)

**What happened:** On July 17, 2026, CIT Senior Judge Richard Eaton ordered CBP to reliquidate "without regard to IEEPA duties" all entries that have been finally liquidated (liquidated more than 80 days ago) for approximately 3,700 CIT plaintiffs. Phase 3 programming was expected live by end of July 2026. CBP filed a progress report on August 4, 2026.

**Important limitation:** This is a court-ordered process for importers who have **active CIT cases** — approximately 3,700 cases. It is NOT a universal CAPE portal feature available to all importers. Importers without active CIT cases still face the standard protest window limitation.

**Eligibility change:** Updated the "past protest deadline" path in `checkEligibility()` to mention Phase 3 and the CIT case requirement in the `reason` text. The `status` remains `"excluded_expired"` and `filingMethod` remains `"litigation"` for conservatism — the action required (consulting trade counsel, filing a CIT case) is essentially litigation.

**John's action needed:** Consider whether to add a UI callout on the dossier page for entries flagged as `excluded_expired` / `filingMethod: "litigation"` noting that active CIT plaintiffs may qualify for Phase 3 reliquidation. This could be a meaningful conversion opportunity for clients who dismissed their claim as time-barred.

## 5. CIT Struck Down Section 122 Tariffs — Now Moot (Informational)

**Source:** CIT 3-judge panel ruling, May 7, 2026.  
Confirmed: [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs), [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/), [Barnes Richardson](https://www.barnesrichardson.com/court-of-international-trade-panel-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs-throws-out-most-state-suits/)

**What happened:** On May 7, 2026, a three-judge CIT panel struck down Section 122 tariffs (Euro-Notions Florida v. United States), finding the Administration's invocation exceeded the statute's authority. The CAFC issued an administrative stay on May 12, 2026, keeping the tariffs in effect during appeal.

**Impact:** The ruling provided relief **only to named plaintiffs**, not universally. The CAFC stay was in effect through Section 122's July 24 expiration, making the case substantially moot. No code changes required; updated `S122-01.notes` to reference this case.

**Bottom line for calculator:** No change to refund eligibility for IEEPA-period entries (Feb 1, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026). Section 122 tariffs were never IEEPA tariffs and were never refundable via CAPE anyway.
