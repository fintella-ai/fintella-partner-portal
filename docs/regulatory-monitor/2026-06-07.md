### 2. Section 122 Tariff — CIT Ruled Unlawful, CAFC Stay in Place — NOTES UPDATED ✅

**Source**: *Burlap & Barrel, Inc. et al. v. United States* (CIT, May 7, 2026); CAFC administrative stay May 12, 2026  
**Verdict**: Not actionable for calculator logic or rates, but notes updated for accuracy

**Timeline**:
- **May 7, 2026**: CIT issued a 2-1 ruling holding the 10% Section 122 global surcharge unlawful. The statutory conditions required to invoke Section 122 (a "fundamental international payments problem") were not satisfied. Permanent injunction against CBP collection — but only for the 3 prevailing plaintiffs: Burlap & Barrel, Inc., Basic Fun, Inc., and the State of Washington.
- **May 8, 2026**: Government appealed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC).
- **May 12, 2026**: CAFC entered an administrative stay, suspending enforcement of the CIT order while the appeal proceeds. Section 122 tariffs continue to be collected from all importers except the 3 plaintiffs.

**Why it does NOT affect the calculator today**:  
Section 122 (HTS 9903.03.01) is the *replacement* tariff, not the IEEPA tariff. CAPE only refunds IEEPA duties (collected Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026). Section 122 duties are outside CAPE scope. The CAFC stay means the 10% rate continues in effect for the general public through at least the appeal resolution (or the July 24, 2026 statutory expiration, whichever comes first).

**Action taken**: Updated `S122-01` notes in the seed file to reflect the CIT ruling and CAFC stay status.

### 3. Euro-Notions CIT Update — Finally-Liquidated Entries Under Litigation — NOTES UPDATED ✅

**Source**: *Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. U.S. Customs and Border Protection* (CIT); multiple law firm reports via SmarTrade, Davis Wright Tremaine, Sheppard  
**Verdict**: Not actionable for `checkEligibility()` logic yet — litigation ongoing

**Timeline**:
- **April 7, 2026**: Judge Eaton designated Euro-Notions Florida as lead case after Atmus Filtration voluntarily dismissed.
- **May 22, 2026**: CBP confirmed ~$85B in CAPE claims accepted; ~$20.6B disbursed.
- **May 27, 2026**: Judge Eaton issued orders requiring CBP Commissioner to explain government's position on issuing refunds for ALL entries — including "finally liquidated" entries (liquidated >90 days ago, past the statutory reliquidation window under 19 USC 1501).
- **May 29, 2026**: Judge Eaton denied DOJ's motion to stay enforcement. DOJ signaled it would appeal immediately.
- **June 7, 2026 (today)**: DOJ appeal deadline. Expected to be filed today.

**The core dispute**: CBP reversed its earlier position and now asserts it lacks authority to issue IEEPA refunds on "finally liquidated" entries without an importer-specific court order. This affects importers whose entries liquidated more than 90 days ago but who filed protests within the 180-day window (the "protest" path in the calculator). If DOJ prevails on appeal, these importers would need their own court judgments rather than relying on the CAPE/reliquidation mechanism.

**Why we are NOT changing `checkEligibility()` logic yet**:  
The `litigation` filing method (entries >180 days post-liquidation) remains correctly classified. The `protest` path (80–180 days) is not yet affected — the dispute is about *finally liquidated* entries (>90 days per 19 USC 1501), which overlaps but is not identical to the 180-day protest window. The appeal outcome will determine whether the `protest` path needs a "requires individual court order" warning flag. Recommend revisiting if/when CAFC rules on the DOJ appeal.

**Action taken**: Updated `capePhase1.legalBasis` in the seed file with a June 7, 2026 note on the CBP reversal and litigation status.
