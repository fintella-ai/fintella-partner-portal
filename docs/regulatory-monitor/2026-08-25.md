### 1. Section 122 Tariff Expired — July 24, 2026 ✅ (data updated)

**What changed**: The 10% global import surcharge under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (HTS 9903.03.01) expired by operation of law at 12:01 AM ET on July 24, 2026 — the 150-day statutory maximum. No presidential action was needed or taken; the tariff simply lapsed.

**Side note**: The U.S. Court of International Trade had also struck down Section 122 on May 7, 2026 in *Oregon v. United States* (and related *Burlap and Barrel, Inc. v. United States*), holding the proclamation didn't properly identify "balance-of-payments deficits" as required by the statute. That ruling was stayed by the Federal Circuit pending appeal. The tariffs expired naturally before the appeal resolved, so the legal challenge is now largely moot.

**Source**: [GHY International on Section 122 expiration](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/us-10-section-122-tariff-in-effect-feb-24-ieepa-tariffs-directed-to-wind-down-de-minimis-suspension-continues/); [Gibson Dunn CIT ruling summary](https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/); [Skadden analysis](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs)

### 2. Section 301 Forced Labor Tariffs — Effective July 24, 2026 ✅ (data updated)

**What changed**: USTR imposed new Section 301 tariffs on July 24, 2026 as the Section 122 replacement, based on a finding that ~60 economies failed to adopt or effectively enforce forced labor import prohibitions.

**Structure**:
- **Lower tier (10%)**: Economies with at least partial forced labor enforcement
- **Higher tier (12.5%)**: Economies with no or minimal forced labor enforcement
- **Coverage**: ~60 economies, ~99.4% of US imports
- **HTS codes**: Economy-specific subheadings within 9903.05.20–9903.05.84
- **Exemptions**: 9903.05.85–9903.05.99 and 9903.06.01–9903.06.21 (USMCA/CAFTA-DR goods, civil aircraft, pharmaceuticals)
- **Special treatment**: EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland, Taiwan use combined-duty-rate structure (not additional ad valorem). EU-origin goods under a separate trade agreement at 15% ceiling effective July 1, 2026.
- **Brazil**: 25% Section 301 tariff (separate, under 9903.05.01, effective July 22, 2026)

### 3. CAPE Phase 2 — Reconciliation-Flagged Entries Eligible (June 29, 2026) ✅ (code updated)

**What changed**: CBP deployed CAPE Phase 2 on June 29, 2026 (CSMS # 69066837). Entries of types 01, 02, and 06 that are **flagged for reconciliation** in ACE — but for which the corresponding Type-09 reconciliation entry has NOT yet been filed — are now eligible for CAPE refunds.

**Key restrictions**:
- Must be an **unliquidated entry** or within **80 days of liquidation** (same Phase 1 window applies)
- CAPE strips IEEPA duties BEFORE the trade files the Type-09 (order matters)
- Once a Type-09 is filed, the underlying entries become ineligible via CAPE
- Type-47 drawback entries and surety-paid, TIB, and warehouse entries remain excluded

**Sources**:
- [CSMS # 69066837 — CAPE Reconciliation Deployment](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/41de055) *(proxy-blocked; info from search snippet)*
