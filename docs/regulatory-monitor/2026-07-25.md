## Findings

### 1. ✅ CRITICAL — Section 122 Tariff Expired July 24, 2026

**Status**: Confirmed expired. Code updated.

The 10% global Section 122 surcharge (Proclamation 11012, eff. Feb 24, 2026) expired by statute at 12:01 AM EDT **July 24, 2026** — the 150-day statutory cap under 19 USC 2132 with no congressional extension. CBP ceased collection as of July 24.

**Legal context**: The CIT had struck down Section 122 tariffs on May 7, 2026 in a 2-1 ruling. The Federal Circuit issued an administrative stay May 12 and a full stay of the injunction June 11, 2026, allowing CBP to collect normally through expiration. The appeal continues but is now largely moot since the tariff expired.

### 2. ✅ CRITICAL — Section 301 Forced Labor Tariffs Replace Section 122 (Eff. July 24, 2026)

**Status**: In effect as of 12:01 AM EDT July 24, 2026. Code updated.

USTR announced final action on July 23, 2026 (Dockets USTR-2026-0265 / USTR-2026-0266). New Section 301 forced labor tariffs apply to goods from **60 economies**.

**Rates**:
- **10%** — economies that have imposed (or committed to impose) a forced labor import prohibition
- **12.5%** — economies without an adequate forced labor enforcement regime

**Coverage**: HTS Chapters 1–97, subject to extensive product exclusions (USTR added 471 additional subheadings after public comment).

**In-transit relief**: Goods loaded on vessel before July 24 and entered before July 28, 2026 are exempt.

**Key distinction for the calculator**: These are **NOT IEEPA tariffs** and are **NOT eligible for IEEPA refunds via CAPE**. They are a forward-looking trade remedy action under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974. Adding them to the seed file is informational context only — refund eligibility logic is unchanged.

**Changes made**:
- `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json`: Added `S301-FL-10` (10% rate, rateType: `section301_forcedlabor`) and `S301-FL-125` (12.5% rate) entries documenting the replacement tariff regime.

**Source**: https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations

### 3. ✅ IMPORTANT — CAPE Phase 2 Launched June 29, 2026 (Retroactive update)

**Status**: Live since June 29. Code updated.

CBP launched CAPE Phase 2 on June 29, 2026, expanding IEEPA refund processing to entries of **type 01, 02, and 06** that are flagged for reconciliation, **as long as the Type 09 reconciliation entry has not yet been filed**.

**Scope**: ~2.8 million additional entries, ~$28.7 billion in potential refunds.

**Critical warning**: Filing the Type 09 reconciliation entry AFTER the underlying entries are accepted into CAPE **permanently forfeits CAPE Phase 2 eligibility** for those entries. The filing sequence matters.

**What remains excluded from Phase 2**:
- Type 09 entries themselves (the reconciliation summary)
- Type 47 drawback entries
- Type 08 USMCA Duty Deferral
- Type 23 TIB entries
- Reconciliation-flagged entries where Type 09 was already filed

**Changes made**:
- `src/lib/tariff-calculator.ts`:
  - Added `isFlaggedForReconciliation?: boolean` and `isReconciliationFiled?: boolean` to `EntryForEligibility` interface
  - Added `"cape_phase2"` to the `FilingMethod` type
  - Added Phase 2 handling block in `checkEligibility()` — entries of type 01/02/06 with reconciliation flag get routed to `cape_phase2` filing method (with warning) unless Type 09 was already filed
  - Added `"excluded_recon_filed"` status for the case where Type 09 was already filed
  - Updated `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` comment to clarify Phase 2 nuance
- `prisma/seed-data/ieepa-tariff-rates.json`:
  - Added `phase2LaunchDate`, `phase2Description`, and `phase3Description` fields to the `capePhase1` section
  - Updated Type 09 exclusion note to clarify Phase 2 rules
  - Updated `additionalRequirements` to distinguish Phase 1 vs Phase 2 reconciliation handling

**Source**: https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/06/cbp-confirms-june-29-2026-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-phase-2-launch/
