## 1. Section 122 Expiry — July 24, 2026 (CRITICAL — 6 Days)

**What:** The 10% Section 122 global import surcharge expires by statute at 12:01 AM EDT on July 24, 2026 — 150 days after it took effect February 24, 2026. The statutory maximum under 19 U.S.C. §2132 is 150 days; extension requires an Act of Congress. No extension legislation is pending.

**Impact on calculator:** The `S122-01` seed entry already has `endDate: "2026-07-23"` which is correct. Rate is 10% — confirmed by FR Doc 2026-03824 (Feb 25, 2026). Trump's Feb 22 social media announcement of 15% was never codified.

## 2. Section 301 Forced Labor Tariff — Proposed Replacement (NOT YET IN EFFECT)

**What:** USTR initiated 60 Section 301 investigations on March 12, 2026 (FR Doc 2026-05151) targeting economies that fail to enforce prohibitions on goods produced with forced labor. Proposed tariff structure:
- **10%** — 15 economies that have adequate forced labor prohibitions or have committed through trade agreements
- **12.5%** — 45 remaining economies

Public comment period closed July 6, 2026. Public hearing held July 7, 2026. Final determination NOT published as of July 18, 2026. The administration intends this to take effect concurrent with Section 122 expiry on July 24, but **no final rule or proclamation has issued**.

**Impact on calculator:** If finalized, this would add new `section301` rate entries covering post-July 24, 2026 imports. These are NOT refundable via CAPE (CAPE covers IEEPA tariffs Feb 2025 – Feb 2026 only). **No seed data changes warranted yet** — monitor for Federal Register publication of final determination.

## 3. CAPE Phase 2 — Launched June 29, 2026

**What:** CBP deployed CAPE Phase 2 on June 29, 2026. Phase 2 extends the automated refund pathway to entries flagged for reconciliation (entry types 01, 02, 06) where the Type 09 reconciliation entry has NOT yet been filed — both unliquidated and entries within 80 days of liquidation. Estimated 2.8 million additional entries worth ~$28.7 billion.

Phase 1 context: $89 billion+ in potential and certified refunds accepted since April 20, 2026 launch.

**Impact on calculator:** The `checkEligibility()` function already marks types 01/02/06 as `cape_phase1` since they are not in `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES`. However, the code comments did not reflect that reconciliation-flagged entries of these types were previously a gray area in Phase 1. Phase 2 removes that ambiguity.

## 4. CAPE Phase 3 — Imminent (Late July 2026)

**What:** Phase 3 will process finally liquidated entries (liquidated more than 80 days before the CAPE refund period began). CBP testimony at June 9, 2026 hearing indicated Phase 3 programming targeted for late July 2026. On July 15, 2026, the CIT issued an order with specific guidance on Phase 3 eligibility.

**Critical restriction:** The government maintains that Phase 3 refunds will only be processed for importers who have filed lawsuits at the CIT. The government is simultaneously appealing the CIT's universal refund order (Euro-Notions Florida v. United States, No. 25-00595) to the Federal Circuit. As of July 1, 2026, a CBP progress report showed 18.1 million entries accepted for liquidation without IEEPA duties.

**Impact on calculator:** The `litigation` filing method is correctly assigned to entries past the 180-day protest window. The July 15 CIT order does not change eligibility logic but confirms that Phase 3 CAPE processing requires a CIT filing — not a change to our exclusion rules.
