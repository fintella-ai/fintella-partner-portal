## 2. Section 122 Expiration & Section 301 Replacement — ACTIONABLE ✅

### What happened

Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 imposed a 150-day maximum cap on the surcharge invoked February 24, 2026. That cap expired at **12:01 AM EDT July 24, 2026** with no Congressional extension legislation in place.

Simultaneously (same minute), USTR imposed new additional duties under **Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. §2411)** based on findings that approximately 60 economies failed to adopt or effectively enforce forced-labor import prohibitions. Investigation launched March 12, 2026; final determination published July 23, 2026.

### Rate structure

| Tier | Rate | Countries |
|------|------|-----------|
| Baseline | **10%** | ~46 economies that enacted/committed to qualifying forced-labor import ban |
| Elevated | **12.5%** | ~14 economies without such a ban |

India was initially in the 12.5% tier but moved to 10% after enacting a forced-labor import ban prior to the July 24 effective date.

### HTS implementation

New Chapter 99 provisions: **9903.05.20–9903.05.84** (country-specific subheadings).  
Exemptions: 9903.05.85–9903.06.21 (USMCA/CAFTA-DR goods, civil aircraft, pharmaceuticals, Chapter 98 goods — mirrors former Section 122 Annex II carve-outs).

### Legal challenges

- **Liberty Justice Center** filed a challenge the week of July 24, 2026.
- **25-state coalition** (led by Oregon, Arizona, California) filed CIT suit August 3, 2026, alleging USTR failed to make adequate per-country forced-labor findings and that this is a repackaged form of the struck-down IEEPA tariffs. Outcome unknown.

### Impact on TIE calculator

Section 301 duties are **NOT IEEPA tariffs** and are **not refundable via CAPE**. The tariff refund calculator's eligibility function (`checkEligibility()`) correctly gates on `IEEPA_START_DATE` / `IEEPA_END_DATE` (Feb 1, 2025 – Feb 23, 2026), so no entries after Feb 23, 2026 are ever flagged as eligible. **No code change needed.**

## 3. CAPE Phase 2 — June 29, 2026 Launch

**Source:** CBP CSMS #69035485 (published June 23, 2026); multiple legal alerts

### What launched

CAPE Phase 2 deployed in the ACE Portal on **June 29, 2026**. It expands Phase 1 to cover:

> Entries of **Type 01, 02, and 06** that are **flagged for reconciliation** (i.e., the Type 09 reconciliation entry has not yet been filed).

Key details:
- **Same 80-day liquidation window applies** — unliquidated entries and entries liquidated within 80 days are eligible; 80–180 days still requires a formal protest.
- **Type 09 itself remains excluded** from CAPE (filing the Type 09 recon first permanently locks the underlying entries out of CAPE for now).
- **Type 47 drawback entries remain excluded.**
- As of August 21, 2026: 272,029 CAPE declarations submitted; 191,494 passing file validations; 26.4 million entries accepted; $132.5B in refunds accepted for processing.
- **Phase 3 temporarily delayed** (CBP confirmed August 25, 2026 — building new validations).

### Impact on TIE calculator

No change to `checkEligibility()` or `EXCLUDED_ENTRY_TYPES` required. Entries of Type 01/02/06 were already NOT in the exclusion set; Phase 2 just formally opens the CAPE pipeline for the reconciliation-flagged subset of those types. The 80-day CAPE Phase-1 window constant is still correct. A future Phase 3 (Type 47 drawback, historically liquidated entries) may require a code change — tracking.
