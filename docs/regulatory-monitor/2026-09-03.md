### 1. 🔴 CRITICAL — Section 122 Tariff Expired July 24, 2026

**What**: The 150-day maximum lifespan for Section 122 tariffs (10% global surcharge, 9903.03.01) expired at 12:01 AM ET July 24, 2026, exactly as the seed data `endDate: "2026-07-23"` recorded. However, `supersededBy` was `null` — this PR updates it to reference the Section 301 forced-labor tariffs that replaced them immediately.

**Additional CIT development**: On May 7, 2026, a divided 2-1 CIT panel ruled Section 122 tariffs unconstitutional (Burlap & Barrel, Basic Fun, and State of Washington). However, the CAFC entered an administrative stay on May 12, 2026 — so Section 122 was collected through its natural statutory expiration on July 24, 2026. This does NOT open a new refund path (Section 122 tariffs are not IEEPA tariffs, and no CAPE path exists for them).

**Code change**: Updated `S122-01.supersededBy` from `null` to `"S301-FORCED-LABOR-10PCT"`.

### 2. 🔴 CRITICAL — New Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs (Effective July 24, 2026)

**What**: USTR took final action on July 23, 2026 under Section 301(b) of the Trade Act of 1974, imposing tariffs on 60 economies for failing to prohibit imports made with forced labor. Tariffs took effect at 12:01 AM ET July 24, 2026 — the exact moment Section 122 expired.

**Rates**:
- **10% tier** (17 economies with partial forced-labor enforcement): Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad and Tobago, United Kingdom
- **12.5% tier** (43 economies with no meaningful enforcement): China, Brazil, Vietnam, Russia, Algeria, Angola, Australia, Egypt, Iraq, Israel, Japan, Kazakhstan, Kuwait, Libya, Morocco, New Zealand, Nigeria, Norway, Oman, Peru, Philippines, Qatar, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Africa, South Korea, Switzerland, Thailand, Türkiye, UAE, Uruguay, Venezuela + others

**HTS codes**: Chapter 99 subheadings 9903.05.20 through 9903.05.84 (each of the 60 economies has its own code). Product-specific exemption codes: 9903.05.96 through 9903.06.21.

**Key exemptions**:
- General: in-transit goods, civil aircraft, pharmaceuticals, aluminum/steel/copper, vehicles, wood products, semiconductors, humanitarian donations, informational materials
- USMCA: USMCA-compliant goods from Canada and Mexico are exempt
- FTA: Product-specific exemptions for UK, EU, Switzerland, Malaysia, Cambodia, Guatemala, El Salvador, Argentina, Bangladesh, Taiwan, Indonesia, Ecuador, Jordan

**Special EU/Japan/Korea/Switzerland treatment**: Rate is calculated net-of-MFN (i.e., applied on top of MFN rate only, not on top of the full duty), so effective additional rate may differ.

**Code change**: Added two summary entries `S301-FORCED-LABOR-10PCT` and `S301-FORCED-LABOR-12PCT` to the seed data. Per-country entries with specific 9903.05.XX codes should be verified against the official Federal Register notice (2026-15181, published 2026-07-28) before adding.

**⚠️ NOTE for John**: These are NOT IEEPA tariffs and are NOT refundable via CAPE. The calculator tracks them for current-duty-burden context (same as Section 122 was tracked). If the calculator is not intended to show post-IEEPA current duties, these entries can be omitted. The PR adds them for completeness.

### 3. 🟡 IMPORTANT — CAPE Phase 3 Delayed (Was August 20, Now TBD)

**What**: CAPE Phase 3, which was originally expected late July 2026 (then rescheduled to August 20), was postponed by CBP on August 25, 2026. CBP is building additional validations to ensure finally liquidated entry reliquidation doesn't inadvertently alter non-IEEPA duties.

**Phase 3 scope**: Finally liquidated entries (the calculator's "protest" and "litigation" paths). ~$11.4 billion in duties at stake. Only available to importers who have filed CIT lawsuits. No new timeline announced.

**Code change**: None made in this PR — the `FilingMethod` values (`protest`, `litigation`) remain correct. The "litigation" path in the calculator is accurate: entries liquidated >180 days ago are CIT-only. Phase 3 will make CAPE available for finally liquidated entries to CIT plaintiffs, but this changes the filing *mechanism* rather than eligibility. Recommend monitoring for Phase 3 launch to add `cape_phase3` as a `FilingMethod`.
