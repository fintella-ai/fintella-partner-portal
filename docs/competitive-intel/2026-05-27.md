# Competitive Intelligence Report — 2026-05-27

**Baseline**: [2026-05-06 report](./2026-05-06.md) — delta-focused, new/changed items highlighted.

---

## Summary

Three weeks after the May 6 baseline, CAPE Phase 1 payments are actively flowing ($35.46B estimated in duty refunds processed as of May 11), raising the competitive stakes considerably — tools that still require broker handoff are feeling pressure to offer self-serve CAPE CSV generation. Three new entrants were confirmed this scan (Alliance Drawback Services, Saltbox, Document.com), bringing the total tracked competitor count to 22+. Gateway Lines was re-assessed and is significantly more capable than previously logged — its i-Sight AI processes CF-7501 PDFs and automatically scans all client shipments for eligibility. The CIT's May 7 Section 122 ruling introduces a potential second-wave refund pool (currently stayed on appeal), which may extend the relevance of this tool category into late 2026. **No competitor has shipped TMS embedding, multi-client broker dashboards, or pre-validated CAPE CSV generation.**

## Market Context Update (Since May 6)

### CAPE Phase 1 — Payments Flowing

As of May 11, 2026 per CBP's court filing:
- **126,237** CAPE declarations submitted since April 20 launch
- **15,123,221** entries passed file + entry-specific validations
- **8,338,081** entries liquidated/reliquidated without IEEPA duties
- **~$35.46B** estimated refund + interest for processed entries
- **1,880** refunds not yet transmitted — ACH account info missing from IOR/broker

First ACH payments issued **May 11–12, 2026**.

**Impact on tools**: The refund pool is real and paying. Urgency for brokers and importers to file remaining eligible entries is high. Tools with deadline countdown and CAPE CSV generation now have direct conversion utility.

### Section 122 Struck Down — Partial, Stayed (May 7, 2026)

The U.S. Court of International Trade ruled the Trump administration's 10% global Section 122 tariff unlawful on May 7, 2026. **Critical caveats**:
- Relief is only for two named plaintiffs (Burlap & Barrel, Basic Fun!) and the State of Washington
- CAFC issued a stay May 12 — Section 122 remains in effect for all other importers during appeal
- If the ruling survives appeal, a second CAPE-like refund process may be needed for $XX–YYB in Section 122 duties

**Impact on tools**: Multiple competitors misrepresent Section 122 as fully struck down. This is a legal positioning risk for any tool claiming Section 122 refund eligibility broadly. Fintella should NOT represent Section 122 as refundable.

### CAPE Phase 2 — Not Yet Announced

CBP has signaled Phase 2 will cover reconciliation entries, drawback claims, and historically liquidated entries, but no timeline or scope has been formally published. Expected "later in 2026."

## Recommended Actions

### 1. Surface the pre-validation story before Phase 2 opens

CustomsGenius generates CAPE CSV at $0.25/line — but with no confirmed pre-validation. With Phase 2 likely coming in late 2026 (covering historically liquidated entries and drawback claims — a larger and more complex pool), **Fintella's pre-validation is the wedge**. Brokers who had declarations rejected in Phase 1 due to CBP validation failures will be sensitized to this problem. Recommended: Publish a one-page "Why Your CAPE Declaration Might Get Rejected" content piece (CBP mod-10 check, 9,999-entry batch limit, IEEPA-eligible HTS flagging, liquidation-status mismatch) — and show that Fintella catches all of these pre-submission. This content both educates and pre-positions against CustomsGenius before Phase 2.

### 2. Reposition the deadline countdown as a Phase 2 readiness tool

The 80-day countdown built for CAPE Phase 1 entries is completing its first wave. Most newly liquidating entries from the April–May 2026 period are now entering the countdown window. **Recommended**: Shift the countdown UX messaging from "Phase 1 urgency" to "Deadline Radar — Phase 1 + Protest Window Tracking." Add a row for 180-day protest deadline for entries that missed CAPE Phase 1 (important for the historically-liquidated universe that Phase 2 will likely target). This extends the utility of the feature and creates continued broker value through the Phase 2 preparation window.

### 3. Add Section 301 / 232 classification to ES-003 analysis (with accurate Section 122 handling)

UNIS, CustomsGenius, and The Trade Lab all classify entries beyond IEEPA. Section 122 is a live legal issue (CIT struck it down May 7 for named plaintiffs; CAFC stay issued May 12 — it is NOT yet refundable for general importers). **Recommended**: Add classification flags for 9903.88 (301), 9903.80/85 (232), and 9903.03 (122) to the ES-003 output, with accurate labels: 301 and 232 = "not refundable under CAPE," 122 = "appeal pending — monitor status, not currently refundable." This closes UNIS's differentiator and protects Fintella from the legal misrepresentation risk several competitors are currently running.

### 4. Monitor Gateway Lines for broker-pivot signals

Gateway Lines' i-Sight AI (CF-7501 upload + automatic shipment scanning) is the closest behavioral analog to a multi-client broker workflow — even if not marketed as one. If Gateway announces a broker portal, multi-client management, or CAPE CSV generation, their feature set would be the most credible challenge to Fintella's core broker tooling. **Recommended**: Check tariff.gatewaylines.com monthly for "broker," "dashboard," or "multi-client" additions.

## Market Observations

- **Pricing landscape is bifurcating**: Free calculators (commoditizing rapidly) vs. managed-services tiers (Alliance CHB: 2% success fee; Flexport: full automation pipeline). There is no competitor charging for the calculator itself.
- **AI intake is table stakes by late 2026**: Flexport, Gateway Lines, and CustomsGenius all have some form of AI document processing. Fintella's planned AI intake needs to ship before Phase 2 opens.
- **Hedge fund claim-sale modeling** is emerging as a distinct sub-feature (TariffClaim.ai, TariffRefundIQ, Document.com). Large importers are being approached by claim buyers. Fintella's referral-to-collect pipeline (calculator → deal → commission) is the correct counter-positioning: don't sell your claim at a discount, capture it through a partner broker.
- **Accounting/CPA channel** is opening (Document.com, AICPA content, PwC Canada). CPAs are advising importer clients on refund eligibility — a referral channel Fintella hasn't explicitly targeted.
